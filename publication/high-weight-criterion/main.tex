\title{Relative arithmetic content in symmetric odd-zeta forms\\
\large Exact reductions, higher-weight decay, and a conditional irrationality criterion}
\author{}
\date{29 October 2026}
\begin{document}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
We separate analytic decay from integral normalization in symmetric Pad\'e
forms for odd zeta values. An explicit rational antidifference and a
polynomial zero count prove full augmented rank for every consecutive
block profile used here. Smith reduction gives an exact formula for the
content of the target and rational rows on the cancellation lattice.
A direct kernel comparison extends the weight-five decay estimate to every
fixed higher weight. An elementary logarithm inequality gives a uniform
exponential margin greater than one third for weights at least seven.
Consequently, nonzero integral forms with subexponential relative arithmetic
cost imply irrationality. The Lean companion proves this conditional
implication, including eventual bounds and arbitrary unbounded indexing,
and an exact integral-projector divisibility criterion.
Signed combinations are also allowed. A target-eliminating cofactor
combination gives an integer lower bound that rules out an overstrong
cofactor-norm saving proposed in the supplied note.
Individual primitive normalization gives a sharper signed-pair criterion,
using each form's own coefficient gcd instead of the common content.
The full rational preimage of integer coefficient pairs identifies the
optimal independent-pair Euclidean primitive cost exactly. An integral
binary form and a unimodular reduction certificate compute that cost;
twenty-one additional Lean theorems verify the underlying minimum and
Gram identities. An elementary all-index coefficient bound gives an
explicit cost envelope. A finite-field translation trace gives a common
prime-product divisor for every lower row at every weight, and improves
that envelope; its exponent still does not close the required budget.
The construction-specific relative-cost bound is a hypothesis, not a
conclusion. Signed pairs provide nonvanishing without a positivity
assumption. This paper does not prove the unconditional irrationality
of every odd zeta value at least seven, or of \(\zeta(5)\).
\end{abstract}

\section{Construction and the arithmetic quantity}
Fix \(s=2r+1\ge5\). Let \(n\ge2r\) be even and select
\(m_j=n-j\), \(0\le j\le r\). Write
\[
 d_n=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,n),\qquad
 T_{s,n,m}(t)=\frac{(n!)^s}{(m!)^2}
 \frac{\prod_{v=1}^{m}(t-v)(t+n+v)}
      {\prod_{h=0}^{n}(t+h)^s}.
\]
The convergent positive sums are
\[
 \cA_{s,n,m}=\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}T_{s,n,m}(k).
\]
Every summand is positive in this range. Partial fractions, reflection
\(t\mapsto-n-t\), and the vanishing total simple-pole residue give
\begin{equation}
 \cA_{s,n,m}=b_{n,m}+
    \sum_{q=1}^{r} a_{q,n,m}\zeta(2q+1).
 \label{eq:expansion}
\end{equation}
The coefficients are rational. Weighted Taylor expansion at each pole
shows
\[
 d_n^{s-(2q+1)}a_{q,n,m}\in\Z,\qquad d_n^s b_{n,m}\in\Z.
\]
For completeness, factor \(T=P_mQ_n^s\), where
\[
 P_m(t)=\binom{t-1}{m}\binom{t+n+m}{m},\qquad
 Q_n(t)=\frac{n!}{\prod_{h=0}^{n}(t+h)}.
\]
Vandermonde expansion shows that \(d_n^\ell\) clears the coefficient
of \(x^\ell\) in \(P_m(-j+x)\). The same statement holds for
\([xQ_n(-j+x)]^s\), since its factors are
\((1+x/(h-j))^{-s}\), apart from an integral leading coefficient.
Thus \(d_n^{s-k}\) clears each coefficient of \((t+j)^{-k}\).
Summing the partial fractions proves the displayed denominator bounds.

Clear all rows by \(D_n=d_n^s\). Let \(C_{s,n}\) be the
\((r-1)\)-by-\((r+1)\) integer matrix of coefficients of the lower
periods \(\zeta(3),\ldots,\zeta(s-2)\), and let \(Y_{s,n},Z_{s,n}\)
be the cleared target and constant rows. Whenever \(C_{s,n}\) has full
row rank, its integral kernel
\[
 \Lambda_{s,n}=\ker_{\Z} C_{s,n}
\]
is a saturated lattice of rank two. For an integral basis \(a,b\), define
\begin{equation}
 \Gamma_{s,n}=\gcd\bigl(Y a,Y b,Z a,Z b\bigr).
 \label{eq:gamma}
\end{equation}
Here and below gcds are nonnegative; we require \(\Gamma_{s,n}>0\)
when dividing by it. This gcd is independent of the lattice basis.

\begin{proposition}[Kernel rank and saturation]
If \(\operatorname{rank}_{\Q}C=r-1\), then
\(\ker_{\Z}C\cong\Z^2\), and this kernel is saturated in \(\Z^{r+1}\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Smith normal form supplies unimodular matrices \(U,V\) with
\[
 UCV=(\diag(d_1,\ldots,d_{r-1})\mid0\mid0),\qquad d_i>0.
\]
The equation \(Cv=0\) says precisely that the first \(r-1\) coordinates
of \(V^{-1}v\) are zero. The final two coordinates are arbitrary integers,
so the final two columns of \(V\) give an integral kernel basis.
If \(kv\) belongs to this kernel, with \(k\in\Z\setminus\{0\}\), then
\(0=C(kv)=kCv\). Torsion-freeness of \(\Z^{r-1}\) gives \(Cv=0\),
which proves saturation.
\end{proof}

At weight five the established adjacent-sign theorem gives
\(x_{0,n}x_{1,n}<0\) for the single lower-period row at every even
\(n\ge2\). That row is nonzero and therefore has rational rank one;
its integral kernel has rank two by the proposition.
For higher weights, counting the rows alone is not a proof of independence.
The following construction-specific argument supplies it, together with
the stronger augmented-rank assertion.

\begin{proposition}[Exact content by Smith reduction]
Let \(\Delta_k(B)\) be the nonnegative gcd of all \(k\)-minors of an
integer matrix \(B\), with \(\Delta_0(B)=1\).
For a full-row-rank matrix \(C\) with \(r-1\) rows and \(r+1\) columns,
\begin{equation}
 \Gamma=
 \frac{\gcd\bigl(\Delta_r(C;Y),\Delta_r(C;Z)\bigr)}
      {\Delta_{r-1}(C)}.
 \label{eq:smith}
\end{equation}
The notation \((C;Y)\) means append the row \(Y\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose unimodular \(U,V\) with
\(UCV=(\diag(d_1,\ldots,d_{r-1})\mid0)\).
The last two columns of \(V\) are a basis of the integral kernel.
The gcd of the maximal minors of \(C\) is \(\prod_i d_i\).
The only possibly nonzero \(r\)-minors after appending \(Y\) are
\((\prod_i d_i)Ya\) and \((\prod_i d_i)Yb\), up to sign.
Unimodular row and column transformations preserve determinantal
divisors. Repeat with \(Z\) and take the gcd.
\end{proof}
Consequently, with the convention \(v_p(0)=+\infty\),
\begin{equation}
 v_p(\Gamma)=
 \min\{v_p(\Delta_r(C;Y)),v_p(\Delta_r(C;Z))\}
       -v_p(\Delta_{r-1}(C)).
 \label{eq:valuation}
\end{equation}
This is a divisibility problem, distinct from the real growth of the minors.

\section{Unconditional augmented rank by rational summation}
\begin{theorem}[Consecutive-block augmented rank]
\label{thm:rank}
For every \(s=2r+1\ge5\) and even \(n\ge2r\), the rational coefficient
columns of the blocks \(m=n-r,\ldots,n\), in the order
\(\zeta(3),\ldots,\zeta(s),1\), are linearly independent over \(\Q\).
Consequently
\[
 \det(C_{s,n};Y_{s,n};Z_{s,n})\ne0,\qquad
 \operatorname{rank}_{\Q}C_{s,n}=r-1.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose a rational combination of these coefficient columns is zero.
Apply its weights to the kernels and write
\[
 F(t)=\frac{H(t)}{\prod_{j=0}^n(t+j)^s}
     =\sum_{q=1}^s\sum_{j=0}^n\frac{c_{j,q}}{(t+j)^q},
 \qquad \deg H\le2n.
\]
Put \(m_0=n-r\). Every block numerator vanishes at
\(1,\ldots,m_0\), so \(F\) does too. Since \(n\) is even and \(s\)
is odd, \(F(-n-t)=-F(t)\). Uniqueness of partial fractions gives
\(c_{n-j,q}=(-1)^{q+1}c_{j,q}\). In particular the total coefficient
\(\sum_jc_{j,q}\) is zero for even \(q\). It is zero for odd
\(q=3,\ldots,s\) by the assumed zero period coordinates. For \(q=1\)
it is zero because \(F(t)=O(t^{-2})\) at infinity. Thus
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{j=0}^n c_{j,q}=0\quad(1\le q\le s),\qquad
 Z(F)=-\sum_{q=1}^s\sum_{j=0}^n c_{j,q}H_j^{(q)}=0,
 \label{eq:zero-residues}
\end{equation}
where \(H_j^{(q)}=\sum_{k=1}^j k^{-q}\). The second equality is the
rational-coordinate hypothesis, not an assumed independence of zeta values.

Define explicitly
\[
 b_{h,q}=-\sum_{j=0}^h c_{j,q},\qquad
 G(t)=\sum_{q=1}^s\sum_{h=0}^{n-1}\frac{b_{h,q}}{(t+h)^q}.
\]
The coefficients of \(G(t+1)-G(t)\) at the first pole, the interior
poles, and the last pole are respectively
\(-b_{0,q}=c_{0,q}\),
\(b_{j-1,q}-b_{j,q}=c_{j,q}\), and
\(b_{n-1,q}=c_{n,q}\). The last equality uses
\eqref{eq:zero-residues}. Hence
\begin{equation}
 G(t+1)-G(t)=F(t).
 \label{eq:antidifference}
\end{equation}
Finite summation and reflection of the prefix sums give
\begin{align}
 G(1)&=-\sum_{q,j}c_{j,q}(H_n^{(q)}-H_j^{(q)})=-Z(F)=0,
 \label{eq:rank-boundary}\\
 b_{n-1-h,q}&=(-1)^q b_{h,q},\qquad G(1-n-t)=G(t).
 \label{eq:rank-reflection}
\end{align}
For the first equality the term with \(j=n\) may be added because its
harmonic difference is zero. For the second, replace a prefix sum by
the complementary tail using \(\sum_jc_{j,q}=0\), then use
\(c_{n-j,q}=(-1)^{q+1}c_{j,q}\). Substitution in the finite expression
for \(G\) proves its reflection identity.

Let \(Q(t)=\prod_{h=0}^{n-1}(t+h)\) and combine the fractions as
\(G=P/Q^s\). If \(P\ne0\), its degree \(d\) is at most \(sn-1\).
Clearing \eqref{eq:antidifference} gives the polynomial identity
\begin{equation}
 H(t)=t^sP(t+1)-(t+n)^sP(t).
 \label{eq:rank-degree}
\end{equation}
Write \(a\ne0\) for the leading coefficient of \(P\). The degree
\(d+s\) terms cancel, and the coefficient of \(t^{d+s-1}\) is
\((d-sn)a\ne0\). Indeed the next coefficient of \(P\) cancels
between the two products; translation of its leading term contributes
\(da\), while multiplication by \((t+n)^s\) contributes \(sna\).
The same formula holds directly when \(d=0\). Therefore
\[
 d+s-1=\deg H\le2n,\qquad d\le2n-s+1=2m_0.
\]

Starting from \(G(1)=0\), equation \eqref{eq:antidifference} and
\(F(k)=0\), \(1\le k\le m_0\), imply
\(G(1)=\cdots=G(m_0+1)=0\).
Reflection supplies the further zeros
\(G(-n)=\cdots=G(-n-m_0)=0\).
These \(2m_0+2\) points are distinct and avoid the possible poles
\(0,-1,\ldots,1-n\). Hence \(P\) has \(2m_0+2\) distinct zeros,
contradicting \(\deg P\le2m_0\). Thus \(P=0\), so \(G=F=0\).

The normalized numerator polynomials of the original blocks have
distinct degrees \(2(n-r),2(n-r+1),\ldots,2n\), with nonzero leading
coefficients. A nonzero rational combination could not cancel its
largest-degree term. All original weights are therefore zero.
The square rational coefficient matrix is invertible. Clearing by
\(d_n^s\), or reversing the column order, preserves rank. Its first
\(r-1\) rows are independent as a subset of the rows of an invertible
matrix, proving the lower-period assertion as well.
\end{proof}

This is a complete rational-function proof for the specified family.
It is the one-pole-orbit discrete-residue approach to rational
summability; the general theory is discussed in
\cite{discrete-residues,discrete-residues-new}. The antidifference,
boundary value, and degree drop needed here have all been derived
explicitly, so no identification with an external family is assumed.
The theorem settles rank and signed-pair nonvanishing. It does not
bound the primitive arithmetic cost.
In particular \(\ker_{\Z}C_{s,n}\) is now unconditionally a saturated
rank-two lattice. For any integral basis \(a,b\), the Smith determinant
identity below gives \(Y(a)Z(b)-Y(b)Z(a)\ne0\); thus an unproved
positivity assertion is not needed to ensure that at least one of the
two primitive signed forms survives.

\section{The exact integer-form bound}
\label{sec:integer-forms}
Suppose \(w_{s,n}\in\Lambda_{s,n}\cap\Z_{\ge0}^{r+1}\) is nonzero.
Put \(M_{s,n}=\|w_{s,n}\|_1\) and
\[
 h(w)=\gcd(Yw,Zw),\qquad
 L_{s,n}=\frac{(Yw)\zeta(s)+Zw}{h(w)}.
\]
The numerator equals \(D_n\sum_j w_j\cA_{s,n,n-j}>0\).
In particular \(h(w)>0\). Since \(\Gamma_{s,n}\mid h(w)\), we obtain
\begin{equation}
 0<L_{s,n}\le
 d_n^s\frac{M_{s,n}}{\Gamma_{s,n}}\,
 S_{s,n},\qquad
 S_{s,n}=\max_{0\le j\le r}\cA_{s,n,n-j}.
 \label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
Both coefficients of \(L_{s,n}\) are integers.
Rational normalization of a kernel vector to mass one does not prove
a bound for \(M_{s,n}/\Gamma_{s,n}\), since clearing its denominator
must still be included in \eqref{eq:bound}.

\section{A direct analytic extension to higher weights}
\label{sec:higher-decay}
The following argument makes the weight dependence explicit without a
new saddle computation at each weight.

\begin{lemma}[Extra powers of the kernel]
For \(n\ge2r\), \(0\le j\le r\), and \(k\ge n-j+1\),
\[
 Q_n(k)\le3^j4^{-n},\qquad
 T_{s,n,n-j}(k)=Q_n(k)^{s-5}T_{5,n,n-j}(k).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The binomial sum and maximality of the central binomial coefficient give
\(4^n\le(2n+1)\binom{2n}{n}\), hence
\[
 Q_n(n+1)=\frac{1}{(2n+1)\binom{2n}{n}}\le4^{-n}.
\]
The function \(Q_n(k)\) decreases for positive \(k\).
Also \(Q_n(k)/Q_n(k+1)=(k+n+1)/k\).
For \(k\ge n-j+1\), \(n\ge2j\), this ratio is at most three.
At most \(j\) backward steps from \(n+1\) suffice.
The identity follows by cancellation of the definitions.
\end{proof}

For comparison of the fixed shifts at weight five, exact factorial
cancellation gives
\begin{align}
 \frac{T_{5,n,n-j}(k)}{T_{5,n,n}(k+j)}
 &=
 \left(\frac{n!}{(n-j)!}\right)^2
 \prod_{h=1}^j\left(\frac{k+n+h}{k+h-1}\right)^6
 \left(\prod_{h=1}^{2j}(k+2n-j+h)\right)^{-1} \notag\\
 &\le (3^6/4)^j. \label{eq:shift}
\end{align}
Indeed the first factorial ratio is at most \(n^j\), each middle ratio
is at most three, and each final factor is at least \(2n\).
The change \(k\mapsto k+j\) carries the summation range onto \(k+j\ge n+1\).

The elementary weight-five estimate, proved in Appendix A, is
\begin{equation}
 \cA_{5,n,n}\le C_0(n+1)^{12}140^{-n},
 \qquad C_0=12\cdot3^{12}\cdot5^{10}e^{10}.
 \label{eq:base}
\end{equation}
It is not a Lean theorem in the present companion. Appendix A also proves
the sharp bound \(\cA_{5,n,n}\le C_0(n+1)^{12}e^{\la_5n}\).
The derivation locates
the unique maximum of
\[
 6[x\log x-(x+1)\log(x+1)]
 +(x+2)\log(x+2)-(x-1)\log(x-1).
\]
Writing \(y=x(x+1)\), its stationary equation is
\(3y^3-4y^2-5y-1=0\).
The maximum occurs below \(1057/1000\), where the resulting exponential
base is below \(1/140\). The complete summation and tail estimates in
Appendix A supply the prefactor in \eqref{eq:base} and certify the sharper
interval \(-4.970794<\la_5<-4.970793\).

\begin{proposition}[Higher-weight raw decay]
For each fixed odd \(s=2r+1\ge7\) and
even \(n\ge2r\),
\begin{equation}
 S_{s,n}\le C_s(n+1)^{12}\exp(-a_s n),\qquad
 a_s=\log140+(s-5)\log4,\qquad
 C_s=C_0\,3^{r(s+1)}.
 \label{eq:raw}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply the lemma termwise, sum, and use \eqref{eq:shift}.
For the shift \(j\), the constant obtained is
\(3^{j(s-5)}(3^6/4)^j\le3^{r(s+1)}\).
The powers \(140^{-n}4^{-(s-5)n}\) equal \(\exp(-a_s n)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Explicit margin, checked in Lean]
For every integer \(s\ge7\),
\begin{equation}
 a_s>s+\tfrac13.
 \label{eq:margin}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The elementary inequality \(2x/(x+2)<\log(1+x)\), applied at \(x=1\),
gives \(\log2>2/3\). Also
\(\log140>7\log2>14/3\) and \(\log4=2\log2>4/3\).
Thus
\[
 a_s>\frac{14}{3}+\frac43(s-5)=\frac{4s-6}{3}
 \ge s+\frac13.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{The precise conditional irrationality theorem}
All limits in this section may be restricted to an unbounded sequence
of eligible even indices. Write \(\log^+x=\max(0,\log x)\) for \(x>0\).

\begin{theorem}[Relative arithmetic cost]
Fix an odd \(s\ge7\). Suppose that the positive integral vectors
in the cancellation lattice and the integer forms in
\eqref{eq:bound} exist along an unbounded sequence of even indices.
Suppose \eqref{eq:raw} holds and
\begin{equation}
 \log^+(M_{s,n}/\Gamma_{s,n})=o(n).
 \label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
Then \(\zeta(s)\) is irrational.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The prime number theorem yields \(\log d_n=n+o(n)\).
For fixed \(s\), the denominator term has upper exponential rate \(s\).
The polynomial factor in \eqref{eq:raw} has rate zero.
By \eqref{eq:cost} the relative arithmetic cost also has upper rate
at most zero. Taking logarithms in \eqref{eq:bound} therefore gives
\[
 \limsup\frac{\log L_{s,n}}n\le s-a_s<-\tfrac13.
\]
Hence the nonzero integer linear forms tend to zero.
If \(\zeta(s)=p/q\), \(p,q\in\Z\), \(q\ne0\), then
\(qL_{s,n}\) is a nonzero integer and has absolute value at least one,
contradicting convergence to zero.
\end{proof}

For clarity, the Lean theorem uses eventual exponential envelopes
instead of a quotient of logarithms. With \(D_n=d_n^s\),
\(W_n=M_{s,n}/\Gamma_{s,n}\), its assumptions are, for every
\(\varepsilon>0\), eventually
\[
 D_n\le e^{(s+\varepsilon)n},\qquad
 W_n\le e^{\varepsilon n},\qquad
 S_n\le e^{(-a_s+\varepsilon)n}.
\]
These are supplied as hypotheses to the theorem, together with
\eqref{eq:bound} and arbitrarily late nonvanishing. Choosing
\(\varepsilon=1/12\) proves the explicit eventual bound
\[
 |L_n|\le e^{-n/12}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The formal theorem permits any index map tending to infinity, including
the even subsequence; no finite-index extrapolation is used.

\section{An integral projector for the divisibility obligation}
At weight five the cancellation row is a nonzero integer row \(x\).
Put \(g=\gcd(x_1,x_2,x_3)\), \(\ell=x/g\), so that \(\ell\) is primitive.
Choose \(u\in\Z^3\) with \(\ell u=1\). Define
\[
 \Pi=I-u\ell.
\]
Then \(\Pi^2=\Pi\), \(\ell\Pi=0\), and
\(\Pi v=v\) whenever \(\ell v=0\). Its integral image is exactly
\(\Lambda=\ker_\Z\ell\).

\begin{proposition}[Universal congruence test, checked in Lean]
For any integer matrix \(F\) with three columns and any \(c\in\Z\),
\begin{equation}
 c\mid(F\Pi)_{ij}\ \text{for every }i,j
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 c\mid(Fv)_i\ \text{for every }v\in\Lambda\text{ and every }i.
 \label{eq:projector}
\end{equation}
The gcd of the entries of \(F\Pi\) is independent of the choice of \(u\),
up to multiplication by a unit.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If the left side holds, write \(v=\Pi v\) and expand \(F\Pi v\)
as an integral linear combination of its columns.
Conversely apply the right side to each column \(\Pi e_j\), which
lies in \(\Lambda\). The equivalence characterizes all divisors of
the entry gcd and is independent of the section \(u\).
\end{proof}
Taking \(F\) to have rows \(Y,Z\) identifies its entry gcd with
\(\Gamma\) in \eqref{eq:gamma}. This formulation accounts for division
by \(g\) before imposing higher-order congruences. For \(c=p^e\), it
is an exact reformulation of the local obligation, not a lower bound
for its exponent.

\section{Shape control and the weight-five boundary}
\label{sec:shape}
The augmented-rank condition used here has the following exact proof.
Choose an integral kernel basis \(a,b\), under the lower-row rank condition,
and put
\[
 B=\begin{pmatrix}Y(a)&Y(b)\\ Z(a)&Z(b)\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Apply the Smith transformations from Section 1 to the augmented matrix.
Its first rows have diagonal entries \(d_1,\ldots,d_{r-1}\), and its
lower right corner is \(B\). Unimodular transformations change determinant
only by a sign. Expansion along the first rows proves
\begin{equation}
 |\det(C;Y;Z)|=\Delta_{r-1}(C)|\det B|.
 \label{eq:augmented}
\end{equation}
Thus the augmented square matrix has full rational rank if and only if
\(\det B\ne0\). Theorem~\ref{thm:rank} now supplies that nonvanishing
for every eligible member of the specified higher-weight Pad\'e family.

Let \(a,b\) be a Gauss-reduced basis of a saturated rank-two cancellation
lattice, \(\la_1=\|a\|\le\la_2=\|b\|\), and
\(H=\|a\wedge b\|\). Set
\[
 U_Y=Y(a)b-Y(b)a,\qquad
 U_Z=Z(a)b-Z(b)a,\qquad
 \cM=\max(\|U_Y\|,\|U_Z\|).
\]
Assume the augmented square matrix \((C;Y;Z)\) has nonzero determinant.

\begin{proposition}[Cofactor comparison]
Under these assumptions,
\[
 \la_2\le\frac{\cM}{\Gamma},\qquad
 \kappa=\frac{\la_2}{\la_1}
       \le\frac{\cM^2}{\Gamma^2 H},\qquad
 \frac{\Gamma}{\la_2}\ge\frac{\Gamma^2}{\cM}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The vectors \(U_Y/\Gamma,U_Z/\Gamma\) are independent lattice vectors.
The second successive minimum is therefore at most
\(\cM/\Gamma\); for a Gauss-reduced rank-two basis it equals \(\la_2\).
Since \(H=\la_1\la_2\sin\theta\) and \(\sin\theta\le1\),
\(\kappa\le\la_2^2/H\). Combine the two inequalities.
\end{proof}

In weight five, positive rounding gives the separate sufficient bound
\[
 M_{5,n}\le3(1+2R_n)\la_{2,n}.
\]
The row-balance result \(\log R_n=o(n)\) removes this factor from
the exponential budget, but not the factor \(\la_{2,n}/\Gamma_{5,n}\).
Using only the explicit base \(140\), a sufficient joint estimate is
\[
 \Gamma_{5,n}\ge e^{0.06n}\la_{2,n}.
\]
The sharper saddle value
\(-4.970794<\la_5<-4.970793\), certified in Appendix A,
reduces \(0.06\) to \(0.03\).
The sharper constant enclosure and this sufficient joint estimate are
not formalized in the new Lean modules. Neither joint arithmetic
estimate has been proved for the constructed family.
In particular this section supplies no unconditional theorem for
\(\zeta(5)\).

\section{Signed cancellation and a cofactor restriction}
For real weights of either sign, the triangle inequality gives
\begin{equation}
 \left|\sum_j w_j\cA_j\right|
       \le\left(\sum_j|w_j|\right)\max_j|\cA_j|.
 \label{eq:signed}
\end{equation}
Thus nonnegativity is not essential to the upper estimate. Its role in
Section~\ref{sec:integer-forms} is to guarantee nonvanishing. A different guarantee is available
for two coefficient pairs: if \(ad-bc\ne0\), the forms
\(a\alpha+c\) and \(b\alpha+d\) cannot both vanish, since
\[
 a(b\alpha+d)-b(a\alpha+c)=ad-bc.
\]
One may therefore work with a pair of independent signed forms, provided
both satisfy the required arithmetic bounds. These two elementary
assertions are proved in the signed Lean companion.

\begin{proposition}[Individual primitive normalization]
Let \(a,b\) be any integral kernel basis, and suppose
\(\delta=Y(a)Z(b)-Y(b)Z(a)\ne0\). Put
\[
 h_a=\gcd(Y(a),Z(a)),\quad h_b=\gcd(Y(b),Z(b)),\quad
 A=\|a\|_2^2,\ B=\langle a,b\rangle,\ C=\|b\|_2^2.
\]
Both gcds are positive. The two primitive integer forms
\[
 L_a=\frac{Y(a)\zeta(s)+Z(a)}{h_a},\qquad
 L_b=\frac{Y(b)\zeta(s)+Z(b)}{h_b}
\]
cannot both vanish. Define
\[
 G^\sharp=\begin{pmatrix}
 A/h_a^2&B/(h_a h_b)\\ B/(h_a h_b)&C/h_b^2
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \tau^\sharp=A/h_a^2+C/h_b^2.
\]
Writing \(N=r+1\) and \(\mu^\sharp\) for the larger eigenvalue,
\begin{equation}
 \max(|L_a|,|L_b|)
 \le\sqrt N\,d_n^s S_{s,n}\sqrt{\mu^\sharp}
 \le\sqrt N\,d_n^s S_{s,n}\sqrt{\tau^\sharp},
 \qquad
 \tau^\sharp\le\frac{A+C}{\Gamma^2}.
 \label{eq:individual}
\end{equation}
No reduction condition on the individually scaled basis is required.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A zero gcd would make the corresponding coefficient column zero,
contradicting \(\delta\ne0\). Dividing each column by its gcd gives
integer coefficients and determinant \(\delta/(h_a h_b)\ne0\).
The preceding determinant identity proves nonvanishing of at least one
form. The signed bound and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\( |L_a|\le\sqrt N\,d_n^s S_{s,n}\sqrt A/h_a\), and similarly
for \(b\). The matrix \(G^\sharp\) is the Gram matrix of
\(a/h_a,b/h_b\). Thus its diagonal entries are at most
\(\mu^\sharp\), and \(\mu^\sharp\le\operatorname{tr}G^\sharp\).
Equivalently these inequalities follow directly from the quadratic
root formula and \(B^2\le AC\). Finally \(\Gamma\mid h_a,h_b\)
implies \(h_a,h_b\ge\Gamma\), proving the last inequality.
\end{proof}

For every fixed \(s\ge7\), a sufficient condition for the nonzero
selected primitive forms in \eqref{eq:individual} to tend to zero is
\begin{equation}
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log\tau^\sharp_{s,n}
       <2(a_s-s).
 \label{eq:individual-rate}
\end{equation}
Indeed the upper exponential rate in \eqref{eq:individual} is
\(s-a_s+\tfrac12\limsup n^{-1}\log\tau^\sharp\).
A rate at most \(1/2\) would suffice at every such weight, since
\(a_s>s+1/3\). This substitution permits additional divisibility
in each selected vector; it does not require a separate lower bound
for \(\Gamma\). Theorem~\ref{thm:rank} supplies uniform augmented
nonvanishing, but \eqref{eq:individual-rate} remains unproved for the
actual family. No Loewner comparison between
\(G^\sharp\) and \(G/\Gamma^2\) is asserted: unequal diagonal
scalings need not commute with \(G\).

\begin{proposition}[All primitive coefficient pairs]
\label{prop:coefficient-lattice}
Let \(\Lambda=\ker_\Z C\), \(V=\operatorname{span}_\R\Lambda\),
and \(\Phi(v)=(Y(v),Z(v))\). By Theorem~\ref{thm:rank}, \(\Phi\)
is a linear isomorphism from \(V\) to \(\R^2\). Define
\[
 \Lambda^{\rm coeff}=\Phi^{-1}(\Z^2)
       \subset\operatorname{span}_\Q\Lambda.
\]
For an integral basis \(a,b\) of \(\Lambda\), put
\(B=(\Phi(a)\mid\Phi(b))\) and \(\delta=\det B\ne0\).
Then \(\Lambda^{\rm coeff}\) is a rational rank-two lattice with
\begin{equation}
 [\Lambda^{\rm coeff}:\Lambda]=|\delta|,\qquad
 \covol(\Lambda^{\rm coeff})=H/|\delta|,
 \qquad H=\covol(\Lambda).
 \label{eq:coefficient-covolume}
\end{equation}
Moreover the optimal independent-pair Euclidean primitive cost is exactly
\begin{equation}
 \min_{\substack{w_1,w_2\in\Lambda\\w_1,w_2\text{ independent}}}
       \max_{i=1,2}\frac{\|w_i\|_2}{\gcd(Y(w_i),Z(w_i))}
       =\lambda_2(\Lambda^{\rm coeff}).
 \label{eq:optimal-pair-cost}
\end{equation}
The minimum is attained. In particular the right side is at most
\(\lambda_2(\Lambda)/\Gamma\), and at most
\(\max(\|a\|_2/h_a,\|b\|_2/h_b)\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In coordinates \(a,b\), the new lattice is \(B^{-1}\Z^2\).
The index of \(B\Z^2\) in \(\Z^2\) is \(|\delta|\), and the
coordinate change multiplies area by \(|\delta|^{-1}\).
This proves \eqref{eq:coefficient-covolume}.

For nonzero \(w\in\Lambda\), put \(h=\gcd(Y(w),Z(w))>0\).
Then \(w/h\in\Lambda^{\rm coeff}\) and \(\Phi(w/h)\) is primitive
in \(\Z^2\). Conversely, if \(v\in\Lambda^{\rm coeff}\) has
primitive image, clear its rational coordinates by a positive integer
\(t\). The vector \(w=tv\) lies in \(\Lambda\) and its coefficient
gcd is exactly \(t\), so \(w/h(w)=v\).
Explicitly, if \(z=\Phi(v)\in\Z^2\) is primitive, take the integer
coordinate vector \(x=\delta\operatorname{adj}(B)z\). Then
\[
 Bx=\delta^2z,\qquad \gcd((Bx)_1,(Bx)_2)=\delta^2,\qquad
 x/\delta^2=B^{-1}z.
\]
This accounts for every denominator in the converse.

Any two independent primitive-normalized vectors are independent vectors
of \(\Lambda^{\rm coeff}\), so their maximum length is at least its
second successive minimum. Conversely choose a Gauss-reduced basis of
\(\Lambda^{\rm coeff}\). Its longer norm equals that second minimum.
Its image under \(\Phi\) is a basis of \(\Z^2\), so both image
columns are primitive; the preceding construction realizes each basis
vector as \(w/h(w)\). This proves the attained equality
\eqref{eq:optimal-pair-cost}. Finally \(\Lambda/\Gamma\) is contained
in \(\Lambda^{\rm coeff}\), and \(a/h_a,b/h_b\) are two independent
vectors in it. These inclusions prove the last comparisons.
\end{proof}

For the reduced basis of \(\Lambda^{\rm coeff}\), the two integer
coefficient forms have determinant \(\pm1\), so cannot both vanish.
The signed norm bound gives, for each form,
\begin{equation}
 |L_i|\le\sqrt{r+1}\,d_n^s S_{s,n}
                    \lambda_2(\Lambda^{\rm coeff}_{s,n}).
 \label{eq:optimal-pair-bound}
\end{equation}
Thus a sufficient arithmetic estimate is
\begin{equation}
 \limsup\frac1n\log\lambda_2(\Lambda^{\rm coeff}_{s,n})<a_s-s.
 \label{eq:coefficient-rate}
\end{equation}
This is a less restrictive optimization than choosing a fixed integral
basis and dividing by a shared content. It is not an estimate established
for the family. In particular, writing
\(\kappa^{\rm coeff}=\lambda_2/\lambda_1\) for this new lattice,
the Gauss angle bound gives only
\[
 \lambda_2(\Lambda^{\rm coeff})^2
       \le\frac2{\sqrt3}\,\kappa^{\rm coeff}\frac H{|\delta|}.
\]
The required quantitative bound on this ratio remains unproved.
Changing the common clearing convention does not create an arithmetic
saving: replacing \(D,\Phi\) by \(cD,c\Phi\), \(c>0\), scales
\(\Lambda^{\rm coeff}\) by \(1/c\) and leaves
\(D\lambda_2(\Lambda^{\rm coeff})\) unchanged.

\subsection{The exact reduced binary form}
The preceding optimization has an explicit two-by-two certificate.
Write the Gram matrix of the source basis as
\[
 G=\begin{pmatrix}A&B_0\\B_0&C_0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 H^2=AC_0-B_0^2>0,
 \qquad K=\begin{pmatrix}y_a&y_b\\z_a&z_b\end{pmatrix},
 \quad\delta=\det K\ne0.
\]
Here \(K\) is the coefficient matrix called \(B\) in
Proposition~\ref{prop:coefficient-lattice}. Put
\begin{align*}
 S&=K\operatorname{adj}(G)K^T,\\
 S_{11}&=C_0y_a^2-2B_0y_ay_b+Ay_b^2,\\
 S_{12}&=C_0y_az_a-B_0(y_az_b+y_bz_a)+Ay_bz_b,\\
 S_{22}&=C_0z_a^2-2B_0z_az_b+Az_b^2.
\end{align*}
This is a positive definite integral matrix and
\(\det S=\delta^2H^2\). Choose an integral unimodular matrix \(U\)
such that
\[
 U^TSU=\begin{pmatrix}\mu&\beta\\\beta&\nu\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 0<\mu\le\nu,\qquad 2|\beta|\le\mu.
\]
\begin{proposition}[Exact minimum certificate]
\label{prop:binary-minimum}
For these data,
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_2(\Lambda^{\rm coeff})^2=\frac\nu{\delta^2},\qquad
 \lambda_1((\Lambda^{\rm coeff})^*)^2=\frac\mu{H^2},
 \qquad
 \frac{H^2}{\mu}\le\frac\nu{\delta^2}
                 \le\frac{4H^2}{3\mu}.
 \label{eq:binary-minima}
\end{equation}
All integer directions are covered by the reduction certificate; this is
not a minimum over a finite search region. Consequently the precise
remaining rate condition is
\begin{equation}
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}
 \frac{\log\nu_{s,n}-2\log|\delta_{s,n}|}{2n}<a_s-s.
 \label{eq:binary-rate}
\end{equation}
The limits here and below run through the eligible even indices.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a reduced form \(Q(p,q)=\mu p^2+2\beta pq+\nu q^2\),
\[
 Q(p,q)\ge\frac{\mu p^2+\nu q^2}{2}.
\]
If \(p,q\in\Z\) and \(|q|=1\), use
\(p^2\ge|p|\) and \(2|\beta|\le\mu\) to get \(Q(p,q)\ge\nu\).
If \(|q|\ge2\), the displayed bound is at least \(2\nu\).
If \(q=0\) and \(p\ne0\), then \(Q(p,0)\ge\mu\).
Thus the first integer minimum is \(\mu\); two independent integer
vectors cannot both have second coordinate zero, so their maximum
value is at least \(\nu\). The coordinate basis attains both minima.
The inverse integral matrix \(U^{-1}\) transports these statements to
every integer vector for the original form \(S\).

The adjoint of \(\Phi\) identifies the dual lattice with
\(\Z P_VY+\Z P_VZ\), whose Gram matrix is \(S/H^2\).
The primal coefficient basis has Gram matrix
\[
 K^{-T}GK^{-1}
   =\frac1{\delta^2}\operatorname{adj}(K)^TG\operatorname{adj}(K)
   =\frac1{\delta^2}\operatorname{adj}(S).
\]
For a two-by-two symmetric matrix, its adjugate is integrally equivalent
to itself by a quarter turn. It therefore has the same two integer
minimum values. This proves the first two equalities in
\eqref{eq:binary-minima}. Finally
\(\mu\nu-\beta^2=\delta^2H^2\), and reduction gives
\(0\le\beta^2\le\mu^2/4\le\mu\nu/4\).
Hence \(1\le\mu\nu/(\mu\nu-\beta^2)\le4/3\), which is the
two-sided comparison in \eqref{eq:binary-minima}.
Equation~\eqref{eq:binary-rate} is exactly
\eqref{eq:coefficient-rate} after taking logarithms.
\end{proof}

In particular the planar transference product lies between \(1\) and
\(2/\sqrt3\). An upper bound by \(1\), without an angle factor, would
reverse the lower inequality. The integer certificate proves the
minimum identities but supplies no uniform estimate for \(\mu/H^2\)
or \(\nu/\delta^2\).

\subsection{An unconditional all-index coefficient and cost bound}
It is useful to compute what follows from elementary height estimates
alone, with no assumed divisibility improvement. Define
\[
 P_s(n)=(n+1)((s+2)n+s)^{s-1},\qquad
 E_s(n)=P_s(n)2^{(s+3)n}.
\]
\begin{proposition}[Explicit elementary cost envelope]
\label{prop:elementary-cost}
For \(0\le m\le n\), every odd-period coefficient in
\eqref{eq:expansion} has absolute value at most \(E_s(n)\).
For the consecutive-block cancellation lattice,
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_2(\Lambda^{\rm coeff}_{s,n})
 \le r!\,d_n^{r(r-1)}E_s(n)^{r-1}.
 \label{eq:coarse-cost}
\end{equation}
Consequently its upper exponential rate is at most
\[
 B_s=r(r-1)+(s+3)(r-1)\log2.
\]
This upper bound is unconditional, but \(B_s>a_s-s\) for every
\(s\ge7\). It does not establish \eqref{eq:binary-rate}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At the pole \(-j\), the leading Laurent coefficient has absolute value
\[
 |c_{j,s}|=\binom nj^s\binom{j+m}m\binom{n-j+m}m
       \le2^{sn+j+m+n-j+m}\le2^{(s+3)n}.
\]
After this nonzero coefficient is factored out, the local Taylor product
has \(2m\) numerator factors \(1\pm x/k\) and \(sn\) inverse
linear factors, each with \(|k|\ge1\), counting multiplicity.
Taking absolute coefficients, it is majorized coefficient by coefficient
by
\[
 (1+x)^{2m}(1-x)^{-sn}\preccurlyeq(1-x)^{-(s+2)n}.
\]
For \(0\le u\le s-1\), the coefficient of \(x^u\) in the last
series is \(\binom{(s+2)n+u-1}{u}\), at most
\(((s+2)n+s)^{s-1}\). Thus every Laurent coefficient is bounded
by \(2^{(s+3)n}((s+2)n+s)^{s-1}\). Summing over \(j=0,\ldots,n\)
proves the asserted period-coefficient bound.

Use the natural integer lower rows
\(d_n^{s-(2q+1)}a_{q,n,m_j}\), \(1\le q\le r-1\).
Their row bounds are \(d_n^{s-(2q+1)}E_s(n)\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:rank} supplies a nonzero \((r-1)\)-column minor.
For each of the two remaining columns, Cramer's rule constructs an
integer kernel vector: its free coordinate is that minor, the other
free coordinate is zero, and its pivot coordinates are signed minors.
It has at most \(r\) nonzero coordinates. The determinant expansion
bounds each minor by \((r-1)!\) times the product of the row bounds.
Both vectors therefore have absolute mass at most
\[
 r!\prod_{q=1}^{r-1}
       \bigl(d_n^{s-(2q+1)}E_s(n)\bigr)
   =r!d_n^{r(r-1)}E_s(n)^{r-1}.
\]
Their two distinct nonzero free coordinates prove independence.
Full augmented rank implies that each nonzero vector has a nonzero
coefficient pair, whose integer gcd is at least one. Primitive division
can only decrease their norms. Proposition~\ref{prop:coefficient-lattice}
now gives \eqref{eq:coarse-cost}.

Since \(\log d_n/n\to1\) and \(\log P_s(n)/n\to0\), the
stated exponent follows. To compare the budgets, use
\(\log140<5\) and \(\log4<2\):
\[
 a_s-s<s-5=2r-4<r(r-1)\le B_s\qquad(r\ge3).
\]
The failed comparison concerns this particular envelope; it is not a
lower bound for the optimal cost and does not exclude sharper savings.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[A sharper coefficient envelope]
Every occurrence of \(E_s(n)\) in the cost envelopes can also be replaced by
\[
 \widetilde E_s(n)=P_s(n)\binom n{n/2}^{s}\binom{3n/2}n^2.
\]
Indeed the central binomial coefficient bounds \(\binom nj\), and
\((j+v)(n-j+v)\le(n/2+v)^2\) for each \(v\). Therefore
\(\binom{j+m}m\binom{n-j+m}m\le\binom{n/2+m}m^2
\le\binom{3n/2}n^2\). The local Taylor majorization is unchanged.
Stirling's formula gives the smaller coefficient exponent
\((s-2)\log2+3\log3\). Thus the resulting cost exponent improves
by a further \((r-1)\log(32/27)\). This refinement also falls short
of the required rate; it is an upper envelope, not a lower obstruction.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[A common prime divisor at every weight]
\label{prop:uniform-prime-divisor}
For odd \(3\le k\le s-2\), define
\[
 G_{s,k,n}=\prod_{n<p\le (s-2)n/(k-1)}p,
\]
where the product is over primes. For every \(0\le m\le n\),
\begin{equation}
 G_{s,k,n}\mid d_n^{s-k}a_{(k-1)/2,n,m}.
 \label{eq:uniform-prime-divisor}
\end{equation}
Consequently \eqref{eq:coarse-cost} improves to
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_2(\Lambda^{\rm coeff}_{s,n})
 \le\frac{r!d_n^{r(r-1)}E_s(n)^{r-1}}
              {\prod_{q=1}^{r-1}G_{s,2q+1,n}}.
 \label{eq:prime-refined-cost}
\end{equation}
If \(H_j=\sum_{i=1}^j1/i\), the resulting upper exponential rate is
\[
 B'_s=B_s-\left(\frac{s-2}{2}H_{r-1}-(r-1)\right).
\]
This is a uniform arithmetic gain; its exponent still exceeds \(a_s-s\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix a prime in the defining interval. Since \(p>n\ge s-1\), we
have \(p\ge s\), and all Laurent coefficients are \(p\)-integral.
In \(\mathbb F_p[t]\), put \(F=t^p-t\). The reduced denominator
of \(T=T_{s,n,m}\) divides \(F^s\), so \(P=F^s\overline T\)
is a polynomial of degree at most \(sp+2m-s(n+1)\).

The translation trace \(\operatorname{Tr}P=\sum_{a\in\mathbb F_p}P(t+a)\)
has degree at most \(\deg P-(p-1)\), or is zero. Indeed, in the
binomial expansion, \(\sum_a a^h=0\) for \(0\le h<p-1\),
including \(h=0\) in characteristic \(p\). Also \(F(t+a)=F(t)\),
and differentiation of \(F'/F=-1/F\) gives
\[
 \sum_{a\in\mathbb F_p}(t-a)^{-\ell}
     =(-1)^\ell F^{-\ell}\qquad(1\le\ell\le s).
\]
The differentiating factorials are invertible, since \(\ell-1<p\).
Write \(a_\ell=\sum_jc_{j,\ell}\) for the raw period-coefficient sum.
Reducing the partial fractions and using translation invariance yields
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}P=\sum_{\ell=1}^s(-1)^\ell\overline{a_\ell}F^{s-\ell}.
\]
On the other hand its degree is strictly less than \((s-k)p\), because
\[
 (s-1)p+2m-sn-s+1
 \le(s-1)p-(s-2)n-s+1
 \le(s-k)p-s+1<(s-k)p.
\]
The powers of the monic polynomial \(F\) have distinct degrees. Starting
with the largest nonzero power shows that all \(\overline{a_\ell}\)
with \(\ell\le k\) vanish. In particular \(a_k\) vanishes modulo \(p\).
The naturally cleared coefficient is an integer; since \(p>n\),
its clearing factor is a local unit. It is therefore divisible by \(p\).
Multiplying the distinct prime divisors proves
\eqref{eq:uniform-prime-divisor}.

Divide each natural lower row by its common divisor \(G_{s,2q+1,n}\).
This preserves its kernel. The same Cramer construction used above now
has the smaller row bounds and proves \eqref{eq:prime-refined-cost}.
The prime number theorem gives
\(\log G_{s,k,n}/n\to(s-2)/(k-1)-1\), so summing proves the
formula for \(B'_s\). At weight seven the improvement is \(7/4\).
For \(r\ge3\), the subtracted contribution is less than \(r(r-1)\);
therefore
\[
 B'_s>(s+3)(r-1)\log2>s-5>a_s-s.
\]
Thus this valid elementary upper envelope does not yet give the strict
upper rate required in \eqref{eq:binary-rate}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A surviving image-content divisor}
The first-order residue congruence also yields a divisor of the actual
image content. Put
\[
 B_{r,n}=\prod_{j=0}^r(n-j)\prod_{j=0}^{2r+2}(3n-j).
\]
Call \(p\) eligible when \(p\le n\), \(p>s\),
\(p^2>3n+2r+3\), and \(p\nmid B_{r,n}\). Define
\[
 P_n=\prod_{p\text{ eligible}}p,\qquad
 A_n=\prod_{\substack{p\text{ eligible}\\
 \lfloor3n/(2p)\rfloor-3\lfloor n/(2p)\rfloor>0}}p.
\]
For the naturally cleared lower rows divided by \(G_{s,k,n}\), write
\(\bar C\), let \(\delta_n=\Delta_{r-1}(\bar C)\), and put
\[
 J_n=\gcd\bigl(\Delta_r(\bar C;Y),\Delta_r(\bar C;Z)\bigr)
     =\delta_n\Gamma_{s,n}.
\]
\begin{proposition}[Surviving content and joint minor saving]
For the actual consecutive-block family,
\[
 A_n\mid\Gamma_{s,n},\qquad P_n^{r-1}A_n\mid J_n.
\]
Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \liminf_{\substack{n\to\infty\\n\text{ even}}}
 \frac{\log\Gamma_{s,n}}n\ge
 c_0:=\frac{\pi}{4\sqrt3}+\frac34\log3-\frac12
 =0.777409057559\ldots.
 \label{eq:surviving-image-rate}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write \(n=pq+a\) and \(m=pq+b\), where \(b=a-j\) for each block.
The exclusions ensure \(a>r\). Let
\[
 L_s(a,b)=\sum_{v=0}^a(-1)^{b+v}\binom av^s
                 \binom{v+b}b\binom{a-v+b}b.
\]
In the ring of \(p\)-integral rational numbers, residue-class splitting
gives
\begin{align*}
 p^{s-k}a_k(n,m)&\equiv L_s(a,b)a_k(q,q)\pmod p,\quad k=3,5,\ldots,s,\\
 p^sb(n,m)&\equiv L_s(a,b)b_{\rm raw}(q,q)\pmod p.
\end{align*}
Here the small raw kernel has no extra factor \(2t+q\), even for odd
\(q\), and its rational boundary uses all Laurent orders.
For completeness, reduce \(p^sT_{s,n,m}(pX-v)\) for \(0\le v<p\).
For \(v\le a\), extracting the factors divisible by \(p\) leaves
the small kernel at \((q,q)\); Wilson's factorial-unit identity
\((pq+a)!/p^q\equiv(-1)^qq!a!\pmod p\) gives its multiplier
\((-1)^{b+v}\binom av^s\binom{v+b}b\binom{a-v+b}b\).
A numerator carry makes both expressions zero, and \(v>a\) gives
zero because the denominator has one fewer complete \(p\)-block.
All factorial arguments are below \(p^2\). Summing over \(v\)
proves the rational-function congruence with multiplier \(L_s(a,b)\).
Taking principal parts proves the coefficient congruences: the order
\(k\) coefficient at each original pole is multiplied by \(p^{s-k}\),
while terms from the other residue classes vanish modulo \(p\).
For the rational boundary use
\[
 H_{h,k}=p^{-k}H_{\lfloor h/p\rfloor,k}+U_{h,k},
 \qquad U_{h,k}\in\Z_{(p)}.
\]
The discarded terms \(p^sc_{h,k}U_{h,k}\) are divisible by \(p^k\).
This proves the rational-coordinate congruence too.

If \(q\) is odd, \(a\) is odd and reflection \(v\mapsto a-v\)
gives \(L_s(a,b)=0\). If \(q\) is even and the defining carry for
\(A_n\) is positive, then \(3a\ge2p\). The boundary exclusions
give \(3a-2p>2r+2\), hence \(a+2b\ge2p\) for every block.
For every \(v\), either \(v+b\ge p\) or \(a-v+b\ge p\), so its
corresponding binomial factor is divisible by \(p\). Thus
\(L_s(a,b)\equiv0\pmod p\) in both cases.
Since \(v_p(d_n)=1\), the entries of \(Z=d_n^sb\) are divisible
by \(p\). The entries of \(Y=d_n^sa_s\) are also divisible by
\(p\), because \(a_s\in\Z\). Therefore \(p\) divides both
coefficient images of every integer kernel vector. This proves
\(A_n\mid\Gamma\) without dividing by lower-row content.

At any eligible prime, the scaled lower rows together with \(Z\)
have rank at most one modulo \(p\): their column multipliers are
\(L_s(a,a-j)\), and their row multipliers are the small-kernel
coordinates. The natural clearing factors and the divisors above
\(n\) only introduce row units. The row \(Y\) is zero modulo \(p\).
An \(r\)-square matrix of rank at most one modulo \(p\) has
determinant divisible by \(p^{r-1}\), as row reduction leaves
\(r-1\) rows divisible by \(p\). At primes dividing \(A_n\),
all rows vanish modulo \(p\), giving \(p^r\). This proves the
joint-minor divisibility.

The small-prime exclusions cost \(O(\sqrt n)\), and primes dividing
\(B_{r,n}\) cost \(O(\log n)\). Hence \(\log P_n/n\to1\).
The positive-carry condition is
\(\{n/(2p)\}\ge1/3\). Apply the prime number theorem to its
intervals and use the Chebyshev bound for the tail. The full interval
rate is
\[
 \frac12\sum_{\ell\ge0}
 \left(\frac1{\ell+1/3}-\frac1{\ell+1}\right)
 =\frac\pi{4\sqrt3}+\frac34\log3.
\]
Restricting to \(p\le n\) removes the interval \((n,3n/2]\), of
rate \(1/2\). Thus \(\log A_n/n\to c_0\), proving
\eqref{eq:surviving-image-rate}.
\end{proof}

This gives a further bound on the actual relative cost. Each coordinate
of a Cramer vector \(w_i\) is an \((r-1)\)-minor, so
\(v_i=w_i/\delta_n\) is an integer kernel vector. Its relative mass
is exactly
\[
 \frac{\|v_i\|_1}{\Gamma}=\frac{\|w_i\|_1}{J_n}.
\]
The two vectors have independent coefficient images. Dividing these
images by \(\Gamma\) gives integer forms; subsequent individual
primitive normalization can only decrease their costs. Using the
central-binomial coefficient envelope \(E_s\) of rate
\(b_s=(s-2)\log2+3\log3\), their maximum relative cost is at most
\[
 \frac{r!d_n^{r(r-1)}E_s(n)^{r-1}}
 {\bigl(\prod_{k=3,5,\ldots,s-2}G_{s,k,n}\bigr)P_n^{r-1}A_n}.
\]
Its upper logarithmic rate is
\[
 r(r-1)+(r-1)b_s-\frac{s-2}{2}H_{r-1}-c_0.
\]
At weight seven this is approximately \(14.995737\), which exceeds
the available sharp budget \(\log2397-7\approx0.781973\).
The new divisor is a proved arithmetic gain, but this upper envelope
still does not prove a negative full rate. Eight exact profiles and
315 local congruences check the formulas. The local and asymptotic
arguments are paper proofs; Lean verifies entry-divisor transfer under
integer pairings and image contents, not the full residue calculation.

However, a signed combination that eliminates the target itself is a
nonzero rational period and has an integral lower bound after clearing.
In the notation of Section~\ref{sec:shape}, put
\[
 \delta=Y(a)Z(b)-Y(b)Z(a)\ne0.
\]
Then \(C U_Y=0\), \(Y(U_Y)=0\), and \(Z(U_Y)=\delta\).
Since \(\Gamma\) divides all four displayed evaluations,
\(\Gamma^2\mid\delta\), so \(|\delta|\ge\Gamma^2\).
Applying the cleared period identity and \eqref{eq:signed} gives
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\Gamma_{s,n}^2
  \le |\delta|
  =d_n^s\left|\sum_j(U_Y)_j\cA_{s,n,n-j}\right|
  \le d_n^s\sqrt{r+1}\,\cM_{s,n}S_{s,n}.}
 \label{eq:cofactorlower}
\end{equation}
Here \(\|U_Y\|_1\le\sqrt{r+1}\|U_Y\|_2\le\sqrt{r+1}\cM\).
The determinant divisibility, signed bound, and integer-divisor lower
bound are proved in \texttt{SignedCancellation.lean}; their application
to these coefficient rows is the displayed paper derivation.

It follows from \eqref{eq:raw} and \(\log d_n=n+o(n)\) that
\[
 \limsup\frac1n\log\frac{\Gamma_{s,n}^2}{\cM_{s,n}}
       \le s-a_s.
\]
In particular, for \(s\ge7\) this upper rate is less than \(-1/3\).
At weight five the coarse base \(140\) gives the upper rate
\(5-\log140<0.058358\); the sharper saddle rate gives
\(5+\la_5<0.029207\).
Consequently the sufficient cofactor strengthenings
\(\Gamma_{5,n}^2\ge e^{0.06n}\cM_{5,n}\), or
\(\Gamma_{5,n}^2\ge e^{0.03n}\cM_{5,n}\) with the sharp saddle bound,
cannot hold along an unbounded sequence with nonzero augmented determinant.
This restriction does not invalidate the comparison in Section~\ref{sec:shape}.
It shows that using its cofactor upper bound in place of the actual
second minimum is too strong for that proposed arithmetic target.
The weaker bound for a selected primitive form remains a separate problem.

\section{Row balance and positive integral cost}
The relative-cost estimate needs arithmetic information about the specified
family. In particular it does not follow from row balance and the existence
of a positive integral kernel vector alone. This can be proved exactly,
without extrapolating from numerical examples.

For an integer \(t\ge2\), take
\[
 C=(t,-(t-1),t),\qquad Y=(1,0,0),\qquad Z=(0,0,1).
\]
The cancellation row is primitive, has full row rank, and its positive to
negative row-sum ratio is \(2t/(t-1)\le4\).
The integral kernel contains \((1,0,-1)\), on which \(Y\) evaluates to one,
so its target-and-constant content is \(\Gamma=1\).
If \(w=(a,b,c)\ge0\) is a nonzero integral kernel vector, then
\[
 t(a+c)=(t-1)b,\qquad k=b-a-c\in\Z_{\ge1}.
\]
It follows that \(a+c=(t-1)k\), \(b=tk\), and
\[
 \|w\|_1=(2t-1)k\ge2t-1.
\]
Equality is attained by \((t-1,t,0)\).
Choosing \(t=2^n\) gives a bounded row-balance ratio but forces every
nonzero nonnegative cancellation vector to have exponentially growing
\(M/\Gamma\). The lower bound, its attaining vector, the row-ratio bound,
and the unit-content implication are checked in Lean.
This family is not the odd-zeta family: it proves a non-implication between
the general structural properties and the desired arithmetic estimate.

\section{A direct combined-kernel construction at weight seven}
\label{sec:combined-seven}
Here is an all-index algorithm for the primitive pair, followed by an
estimate of its combined kernel. This completes the construction and its
normalization; convergence of the finite signed part below is not proved.
Let \(n\ge6\) be even, \(D=d_n^7\), and let \(M\) be the four-by-four
integer matrix whose rows are the cleared coefficients of
\(\zeta(3),\zeta(5),\zeta(7),1\). Its columns are the four blocks
\(m=n,n-1,n-2,n-3\). Theorem~\ref{thm:rank} gives \(\det M\ne0\).
Let \(e_Y,e_Z\) be the final two coordinate vectors and set
\[
 a=\operatorname{adj}(M)e_Y,\qquad b=\operatorname{adj}(M)e_Z,
 \qquad \delta=\det M.
\]
Reduce the positive integral Gram form of \(a,b\) by an integer
unimodular matrix \(U\). For either column \((p,q)^T\) of \(U\), define
\[
 v=(pa+qb)/\delta,\qquad
 t=\operatorname{lcm}\{\text{denominators of the coordinates of }v\},
 \qquad w=tv.
\]
All these operations are exact rational or integer operations at each
eligible index. The inverse-matrix identities give
\[
 Mv=(0,0,p,q)^T,\qquad Mw=(0,0,tp,tq)^T.
\]
Since a column of a unimodular matrix is primitive,
\(\gcd(tp,tq)=t\). Thus \(w\) is an integer cancellation vector,
its coefficient content is exactly \(t\), and its primitive form is
\[
 L_{n,i}=p_i\zeta(7)+q_i=D\sum_jv_{i,j}\cA_{7,n,n-j}.
\]
The two coefficient columns have determinant \(\pm1\). Consequently
these two forms cannot both vanish, at every eligible index. Reduction
also ensures \(\|v_i\|_2\le\lambda_2(\Lambda^{\rm coeff})\).
No lower bound for a separately chosen shared gcd is needed in this
construction; the actual cost remains to be estimated.

Put \(m_0=n-3\), \(u=t(t+n)\), and define the cubic
\begin{equation}
 Q_{n,i}(u)=\sum_{j=0}^3 v_{i,j}
       \left(\frac{m_0!}{(n-j)!}\right)^2
       \prod_{h=m_0+1}^{n-j}\bigl(u-h(n+h)\bigr).
 \label{eq:combined-cubic}
\end{equation}
The empty product is one. The four products have distinct degrees
\(3,2,1,0\), so the cubic is nonzero. Factor cancellation proves
\begin{equation}
 L_{n,i}=D\sum_{k=n-2}^{\infty}
       T_{7,n,n-3}(k)Q_{n,i}(k(k+n)).
 \label{eq:combined-seven-sum}
\end{equation}
Indeed \((t-h)(t+n+h)=u-h(n+h)\), and the initial terms of each
longer block vanish at its positive integer zeros. Thus this identity
preserves the signed cancellation before any norm bound is applied.
The cubic expansion, reflection identity, triangular coefficient
injectivity, and scalar absolute bound are checked in
\texttt{CombinedKernelCubic.lean}.

\begin{proposition}[A uniform combined-tail bound]
\label{prop:combined-tail}
Write \(Q_{n,i}(u)=\sum_{\ell=0}^3q_{\ell,n,i}u^\ell\).
For \(K\ge\max(2m_0,m_0+1)\) with
\[
 K(7(n+1)-2m_0-8)\ge2m_0^2+7n(n+1),
\]
the absolute tail of \eqref{eq:combined-seven-sum} from \(K\) onward
is at most
\begin{equation}
 R_{n,i}(K)=D T_{7,n,n-3}(K)
  \sum_{\ell=0}^3|q_{\ell,n,i}|[K(K+n)]^\ell
                     \left(1+\frac K{7-2\ell}\right).
 \label{eq:combined-tail-bound}
\end{equation}
For the constructed reduced pair, \(R_{n,i}(n^2)\to0\), and in fact
\[
 \limsup\frac{\log R_{n,i}(n^2)}{n\log n}\le-3.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Logarithmic differentiation, using the same reciprocal inequalities as
in Appendix A, shows that \(t^8T_{7,n,m_0}(t)\) is nonincreasing for
\(t\ge K\). Thus \(T(k)\le T(K)(K/k)^8\). Also
\(k(k+n)\le(1+n/K)k^2\), and integral comparison gives
\[
 \sum_{k=K}^{\infty}(K/k)^{8-2\ell}
       \le1+K/(7-2\ell).
\]
These inequalities prove \eqref{eq:combined-tail-bound}.

For \(K=n^2\), all cutoff conditions hold for \(n\ge6\).
Let \(A_n=\|v_i\|_1\). Each factorial ratio in
\eqref{eq:combined-cubic} is at most one and every root has absolute
value at most \(2n^2\). Consequently
\(\sum_\ell|q_{\ell,n,i}|\le27n^6A_n\).
Since \(K(K+n)\le2n^4\) and \(1+K\le2n^2\), the sum in
\eqref{eq:combined-tail-bound} is at most \(432n^{20}A_n\).
Directly from the product formula, discarding the factorial denominator,
\[
 T_{7,n,n-3}(n^2)
 \le\frac{n^{7n}(2n^2)^{2n}}{(n^2)^{7(n+1)}}
 =4^n n^{-3n-14}.
\]
Thus \(R_{n,i}(n^2)\le432n^6D A_n4^n n^{-3n}\).
The unconditional cost envelope in
Proposition~\ref{prop:elementary-cost} gives \(\log A_n=O(n)\),
because \(A_n\le2\lambda_2(\Lambda^{\rm coeff})\).
Also \(\log D=O(n)\). Taking logarithms proves the stated bound.
\end{proof}

The exact remaining analytic assertion for this approach is therefore
\begin{equation}
 D\sum_{k=n-2}^{n^2-1}
       T_{7,n,n-3}(k)Q_{n,i}(k(k+n))\longrightarrow0
       \qquad(i=1,2).
 \label{eq:combined-finite-target}
\end{equation}
The tail theorem does not imply this finite signed estimate. At
\(n=6,8,12,20,40\), exact joint-kernel intervals enclose both primitive
forms strictly between \(-1\) and \(1\) and exclude zero. Independent
Euler--Maclaurin intervals for \(p_i\zeta(7)+q_i\) lie inside them.
These are reproducible checks, not a proof of
\eqref{eq:combined-finite-target}. The construction, normalization,
and tail estimate above are all-index paper arguments. The actual
kernel identity and the tail convergence are not yet formalized in Lean.

\subsection{An elementary saddle and cubic-moment estimate}
The following estimate retains the actual primitive cubic coefficients.
It sharpens the weight-seven raw rate without invoking an unproved
arithmetic estimate. Define
\[
 J_{n,i}=\sum_{\ell=0}^3|q_{\ell,n,i}|(12n^2)^\ell,
 \qquad C_7=12\cdot5^{14}\cdot3^{38}.
\]
\begin{proposition}[Elementary combined-form majorant]
\label{prop:elementary-seven}
For every even \(n\ge6\) and either constructed primitive form,
\begin{equation}
 |L_{n,i}|\le C_7(n+1)^{16}d_n^7 J_{n,i}\,2397^{-n}.
 \label{eq:elementary-seven}
\end{equation}
In particular, an upper exponential rate for \(J_{n,i}\) strictly less
than \(\log2397-7>0.78\) would prove its decay. Such a rate for the
actual constructed coefficients is not proved here.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For the top block, put
\[
 \Phi_7(x)=8[x\log x-(x+1)\log(x+1)]
 +(x+2)\log(x+2)-(x-1)\log(x-1),\qquad x>1.
\]
The negative of the numerator determining the derivative sign is
\[
 (x-1)(x+1)^8-x^8(x+2)=p(x(x+1)),\qquad
 p(y)=5y^4-2y^3-13y^2-7y-1.
\]
Since
\(p(2+z)=5z^4+38z^3+95z^2+77z-3\), it is strictly increasing
for \(z\ge0\), starts negative, and tends to infinity. Thus \(\Phi_7\)
has exactly one maximum \(x_*>1\). At that maximum,
\[
 e^{\Phi_7(x_*)}=q(x_*),\qquad
 q(x)=\frac{(x+2)^2(x-1)}{(x+1)^8}.
\]
Take \(a=25309/25000\). Exact arithmetic gives
\[
 25000^8p(a(a+1))=90142217314698247789970310961605>0
\]
and
\[
 25000^8\bigl((a+1)^8-2397(a+2)^2(a-1)\bigr)
 =13713779341737211888407277469092321>0.
\]
Hence \(x_*<a\). On \((1,a]\),
\((\log q)'=2/(x+2)+1/(x-1)-8/(x+1)>25000/309-4>0\).
Consequently \(\Phi_7(x)\le\Phi_7(x_*)<-\log2397\) for all \(x>1\).

Let \(P_n=\max_{k\ge n+1}T_{7,n,n}(k)\). The derivative bound
for \(t^2T_{7,n,n}(t)\) confines its maximizing integer to \(k\le3n\).
Using \(f(u)=u\log u-u\), the factorial inequalities of Appendix A
applied to
\[
 T_{7,n,n}(k)=
 \frac{(n!)^5((k-1)!)^8(k+2n)!}
 {(k-n-1)!((k+n)!)^8}
\]
give fourteen numerator error terms, each at most \(1+\log(5n+1)\).
Replacing the shifted arguments \(k-1,k-n-1\) by \(k,k-n\)
adds at most \(\log(2n)\), exactly as in Appendix A. Therefore
\[
 P_n\le2e^{14}n(5n+1)^{14}2397^{-n}.
\]
The fixed-offset product comparison gives, at every \(k\ge n-2\),
\[
 T_{7,n,n-3}(k)\le\frac{3^{24}}{64}T_{7,n,n}(k+3)
 \le\frac{3^{24}}{64}P_n.
\]
Here the three quotient factors are at most three for \(n\ge6\);
the factorial and six remaining denominator factors give \(4^{-3}\).

For \(K=3n\), the moment cutoff in Proposition~\ref{prop:combined-tail}
holds. Bounding the initial sum by its maximum and the tail by that
proposition shows, for \(0\le\ell\le3\),
\[
 \sum_{k=n-2}^{\infty}T_{7,n,n-3}(k)[k(k+n)]^\ell
 \le(6n+1)\frac{3^{24}}{64}P_n(12n^2)^\ell.
\]
Indeed the initial part has fewer than \(3n\) terms with
\(k(k+n)\le12n^2\), and the tail factor is at most \(1+3n\).
Insert the bound for \(P_n\), use \(e<3\), and enlarge the constant
to \(C_7\). Summing against \(|q_{\ell,n,i}|\) proves
\eqref{eq:elementary-seven}.

Finally \(\log d_n/n\to1\), and the polynomial prefactor has zero
exponential rate. If \(\limsup\log J_{n,i}/n<\log2397-7\), the
logarithm of the right side of \eqref{eq:elementary-seven}, divided by
\(n\), has a strictly negative upper limit. This proves the stated
sufficient implication. An exact rational logarithm enclosure verifies
\(\log2397-7>0.78\).
\end{proof}

The saddle factorization, shifted polynomial, rational endpoint and
base inequalities, and moment cutoff are checked in
\texttt{WeightSevenElementaryBounds.lean}. The full factorial and moment
derivation above remains a paper proof. The coefficient factor \(J_{n,i}\)
is retained in the bound. It cannot be dropped on the basis of the raw
kernel's decay. Exact evaluations verify the elementary upper bound is
less than one for both forms at \(n=40\); they do not establish its
eventual rate.

\subsection{An exact integer formula for the cubic height}
Write \(x=\delta v\in\Z^4\) for the Cramer numerator used in constructing
one reduced form in Section~\ref{sec:combined-seven}. Put
\[
 A=(n-2)(2n-2),\quad B=(n-1)(2n-1),\quad C=2n^2,
 \qquad F=n^2(n-1)^2(n-2)^2.
\]
Define the integral vector \(b=R_nx\), where
\[
 R_n=\begin{pmatrix}
 -ABC&ABn^2&-An^2(n-1)^2&F\\
 AB+AC+BC&-(A+B)n^2&n^2(n-1)^2&0\\
 -(A+B+C)&n^2&0&0\\
 1&0&0&0
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The cubic coefficients and their primitive height satisfy the exact
identities
\begin{equation}
 q_{\ell,n,i}=\frac{b_\ell}{F\delta},\qquad
 J_{n,i}=\frac{g_b}{F|\delta|}
          \sum_{\ell=0}^3|b_\ell/g_b|(12n^2)^\ell,
 \quad g_b=\gcd(b_0,b_1,b_2,b_3)>0.
 \label{eq:integer-cubic-height}
\end{equation}
They follow by expanding \eqref{eq:combined-cubic}, since multiplication
by \(F\) clears all four factorial ratios.

\begin{proposition}[Integral content and coefficient norm comparison]
\label{prop:cubic-content}
For \(g_x=\gcd(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3)>0\),
\begin{equation}
 \frac{g_b}{g_x}\in\Z_{>0},\qquad \frac{g_b}{g_x}\mid F.
 \label{eq:cubic-content-ratio}
\end{equation}
In particular \(v_p(g_b)=v_p(g_x)\) whenever
\(p\nmid n(n-1)(n-2)\). Furthermore, for every rational vector \(v\)
and its cubic profile,
\begin{equation}
 \|v\|_1\le J\le43904\|v\|_1.
 \label{eq:cubic-mass-equivalence}
\end{equation}
For the constructed reduced pair this gives
\[
 \lambda_2(\Lambda^{\rm coeff})\le\max_iJ_{n,i}
       \le87808\lambda_2(\Lambda^{\rm coeff}).
\]
Thus the cubic-height and optimal-pair conditions have exactly the same
upper exponential rate. Equation~\eqref{eq:integer-cubic-height} is an
explicit arithmetic target, not a bound establishing the required rate.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The monic Newton-basis change in \(R_n\) is unimodular over \(\Z\).
Explicitly, its inverse recovers
\begin{align*}
 x_0&=b_3,\\
 n^2x_1&=b_2+(A+B+C)b_3,\\
 n^2(n-1)^2x_2&=b_1+(A+B)b_2+(A^2+AB+B^2)b_3,\\
 Fx_3&=b_0+Ab_1+A^2b_2+A^3b_3.
\end{align*}
Hence \(g_b\) equals the gcd of
\(x_0,n^2x_1,n^2(n-1)^2x_2,Fx_3\).
All scales divide \(F\). Therefore \(g_x\mid g_b\), while
\(g_b\mid Fx_j\) for every \(j\), and Bezout gives
\(g_b\mid Fg_x\). This proves \eqref{eq:cubic-content-ratio}.
The inverse identities and the equivalent common-divisibility test
for every integer modulus are checked in \texttt{CubicArithmeticFrame.lean}.

For the norm estimate, set \(z=u/n^2\) and
\(\alpha=A/n^2,\beta=B/n^2,\gamma=C/n^2\), all in \([0,2]\).
The scaled cubic has monomial coefficients \(c_\ell=q_\ell n^{2\ell}\)
and Newton coefficients
\[
 d_0=\frac{n^4v_0}{(n-1)^2(n-2)^2},\quad
 d_1=\frac{n^4v_1}{(n-1)^2(n-2)^2},\quad
 d_2=\frac{n^2v_2}{(n-2)^2},\quad d_3=v_3.
\]
Each multiplier lies between one and sixteen for \(n\ge6\).
The inverse Newton formulas and \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\le2\) give
\[
 \sum_j|v_j|\le\sum_j|d_j|
 \le|c_0|+3|c_1|+9|c_2|+27|c_3|
 \le\sum_\ell12^\ell|c_\ell|=J.
\]
Conversely each linear Newton factor has weighted coefficient norm
at most \(12+2=14\), so
\(J\le14^3\sum_j|d_j|\le16\cdot14^3\|v\|_1\).
This proves \eqref{eq:cubic-mass-equivalence}. For the reduced pair,
\(\max_i\|v_i\|_2=\lambda_2\) and
\(\|v_i\|_2\le\|v_i\|_1\le2\|v_i\|_2\), proving the last comparison.
\end{proof}

Combining this proposition with the proved prime-refined envelope gives
the unconditional all-index arithmetic upper bound
\[
 J_{n,i}\le526848\,
 \frac{d_n^6\bigl(P_7(n)\binom n{n/2}^7\binom{3n/2}n^2\bigr)^2}
 {G_{7,3,n}G_{7,5,n}},\qquad P_7(n)=(n+1)(9n+7)^6.
\]
Its upper exponential rate is
\(17/4+10\log2+6\log3\), approximately \(17.7732\).
This is a valid envelope but does not prove a rate below
\(\log2397-7\). Improving prime counts near a fixed finite number of
boundaries cannot change this leading rate when the valuation weights
are bounded: intervals of length \(O(n^\theta)\), \(\theta<1\),
contain at most \(O(n^\theta)\) integers, and their total weighted
log-prime contribution is \(O(n^\theta\log n)=o(n)\).
This statement concerns those boundary contributions only. Any use of
short-interval prime estimates for a new positive main-term saving still
requires a proved valuation formula for the actual coefficients or minors.

\section{Shared parameters and an alternative numerator-length family}
\label{sec:parametric-profiles}
The preceding constructions admit common parameters. Fix \(s=2r+1\ge7\),
even \(n\ge2r\), and an independent integer \(r\le B\le n\).
Use the blocks \(m_j=B-j\), \(0\le j\le r\), with \(m_0^{\rm base}=B-r\).
The case \(B=n\) is the original family; allowing \(B<n\) changes its
numerator zeros, saddle, and coefficient lattice.

\begin{proposition}[Parametric rank and primitive profiles]
The augmented coefficient matrix for these blocks has rank \(r+1\).
Its lower-period kernel has rank two. The adjugate and Gauss algorithm
of Section~\ref{sec:combined-seven} constructs two primitive forms
\(p_i\zeta(s)+q_i\) whose coefficient pairs have determinant \(\pm1\).
Writing \(v_i\) for their rational cancellation vectors and \(D=d_n^s\),
their exact combined profile is the degree-at-most-\(r\) polynomial
\begin{equation}
 Q_{s,n,B,i}(u)=\sum_{j=0}^r v_{i,j}
 \left(\frac{(B-r)!}{(B-j)!}\right)^2
 \prod_{h=B-r+1}^{B-j}(u-h(n+h)).
 \label{eq:parametric-profile}
\end{equation}
It is nonzero and satisfies
\[
 p_i\zeta(s)+q_i=D\sum_{k=B-r+1}^{\infty}
 T_{s,n,B-r}(k)Q_{s,n,B,i}(k(k+n)).
\]
The individual integer lift has coefficient gcd exactly equal to the
least denominator of the coordinates of \(v_i\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Repeat the rational-antidifference proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:rank}.
The combined numerator now has degree at most \(2B\), so the numerator
of its antidifference has degree at most \(2B-s+1=2(B-r)\).
The common integer zeros, boundary value, and reflection supply
\(2(B-r)+2\) distinct roots. Therefore it vanishes. The original block
numerators have distinct degrees \(2B,2B-2,\ldots,2B-2r\), proving
full augmented rank. Since \(B\le n\), all blocks remain proper and
the same denominator theorem applies. The inverse-image lattice, Gauss
reduction, and primitive-lift argument then apply in \(\Q^{r+1}\).
Factoring the common smallest block gives
\eqref{eq:parametric-profile}; the Newton products have distinct degrees,
so a nonzero coefficient vector cannot give the zero polynomial.
\end{proof}

Write \(Q=\sum_{\ell=0}^r q_\ell u^\ell\), and define
\[
 J_{s,n,B,i}=\sum_{\ell=0}^r|q_\ell|(12n^2)^\ell,\qquad
 F_B=\left(\frac{B!}{(B-r)!}\right)^2.
\]
Multiplication by \(F_B\) makes the profile transformation integral:
its \(j\)-th column is the coefficient vector of
\[
 \left(\frac{B!}{(B-j)!}\right)^2
       \prod_{h=B-r+1}^{B-j}(u-h(n+h)).
\]
Its determinant, up to sign, is the product of those integer scales.
The monic Newton change is unimodular. Hence the ratio of its output
content to its input content divides \(F_B\), exactly as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:cubic-content}. This proves the corresponding
prime-power statement in every degree on paper.

For \(B=n\), coefficient norm comparison gives
\begin{equation}
 \|v_i\|_1\le J_{s,n,n,i}\le56^r\|v_i\|_1.
 \label{eq:parametric-norm}
\end{equation}
For \(r\le B\le n\) the upper constant may be replaced by
\((14n^2)^r\); this is polynomial in \(n\) at every fixed weight.
To verify the lower bound, scale \(u=n^2z\). All Newton roots lie in
\([0,2]\), and the factorial multipliers of the scaled Newton coefficients
are at least one. The coefficient of the \(k\)-th Newton basis polynomial
is \(\sum_{\ell=k}^r c_\ell h_{\ell-k}(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_{k+1})\),
where \(h_j\) is the complete homogeneous polynomial. The bound
\(h_{\ell-k}\le\binom\ell k2^{\ell-k}\) gives total inverse coefficient
norm at most \(\sum_\ell3^\ell|c_\ell|\le J\).
The upper bound follows by multiplying the weighted norms of the
linear factors; each is at most \(14\). For \(B=n\) the scaled factorial
multiplier is at most \(4^r\). A dummy additional root may be used in
the top Newton coefficient, where only \(h_0=1\) occurs.

\subsection{What the weight shift proves}
For the normalized kernels there is an exact identity
\begin{equation}
 T_{s+2,n,m}(t)=T_{s,n,m}(t)
       \left(\frac{n!}{\prod_{h=0}^n(t+h)}\right)^2.
 \label{eq:parametric-weight-shift}
\end{equation}
At numerator length \(B\sim\beta n\), the associated phase is
\begin{align*}
 \Phi_s(x;\beta)={}&-(s+1)E(x)
 +(x+1+\beta)\log(x+1+\beta)\\
 &-(x-\beta)\log(x-\beta)-2\beta\log\beta,\\
 E(x)={}&(x+1)\log(x+1)-x\log x,
 \qquad x>\beta>0.
\end{align*}
Thus \(\Phi_{s+2}=\Phi_s-2E\). At \(\beta=1\),
\(E'(x)=\log(1+1/x)>0\) and \(E(1)=\log4\), so
\[
 \Phi_{s+2}(x;1)\le\Phi_s(x;1)-2\log4.
\]
The actual rational-kernel identity, phase identity, derivative,
entropy lower bound, and phase comparison are proved in
\texttt{ParametricWeightShift.lean}. Its normalized definition includes
the poised parity factor and agrees with \(T\) when \(n\) is even.
The same module proves the factorial-normalized block-length identity
used to factor each combined profile, for arbitrary natural parameters.

Put \(\beta_s=2397\cdot4^{s-7}\) and
\(A_s=\log\beta_s\). The elementary factorial and positive-moment proof
of Proposition~\ref{prop:elementary-seven} now gives, at \(B=n\),
\begin{equation}
 |p_i\zeta(s)+q_i|
 \le C_s(n+1)^{2s+2}d_n^sJ_{s,n,n,i}\,\beta_s^{-n},
 \quad C_s=12\cdot5^{2s}3^{2s+(s+1)r}.
 \label{eq:parametric-bound}
\end{equation}
There are \(2s\) factorial-error terms. The fixed-offset comparison
cost is at most \(3^{(s+1)r}/4^r\). For the moments through degree
\(r\), use \(t^{2r+2}T_{s,n,n-r}(t)\) and cutoff \(3n\);
the resulting denominators \(2r+1-2\ell\) are at least one.
These observations prove \eqref{eq:parametric-bound} by precisely the
factorial and moment inequalities already displayed, with the stated
enlarged constant. In particular
\[
 (A_{s+2}-(s+2))-(A_s-s)=2\log4-2>2/3.
\]
The raw analytic margin therefore improves with weight. The required
arithmetic assertion is still
\(\limsup_n\log J_{s,n,n,i}/n<A_s-s\), separately at each fixed weight.
Equation~\eqref{eq:parametric-weight-shift} does not assert a polynomial
bound on the change of this primitive coefficient height: the
lower-period constraints and the coefficient lattice change with \(s\).

For the alternative family, even with \(B=B_n\) varying between \(r\)
and \(n\), truncation at \(K=n^2\) has the unconditional bound
\[
 R_{s,n,B,i}(n^2)\le
 2\cdot6^r n^{2r}d_n^s\|v_i\|_1\,4^n n^{-(s-4)n}.
\]
Indeed the moment exponent is \(2r+2\), the profile coefficient norm
is at most \(3^rn^{2r}\|v_i\|_1\), and discarding the factorial
denominator gives \(T_{s,n,B-r}(n^2)\le4^n n^{-(s-4)n-2s}\).
The elementary Cramer envelope bounds \(\log\|v_i\|_1=O_s(n)\)
uniformly over these \(B\). Thus the tail tends to zero faster than
exponentially for every fixed odd \(s\ge7\). Decay of the corresponding
finite signed sum, or an adequate bound for \(J\), is not proved by
this tail estimate. The shared parameters and alternative family do
not yet establish the unconditional all-odd irrationality theorem.

\section{A separate single-target derivative family}
The following construction eliminates the lower-period lattice entirely.
Fix \(r\ge2\), \(s=2r+1\), \(m\ge1\), and \(n\ge rm\), and put
\[
 R(t)=\frac{(n!)^2}{(m!)^{2r}}
       \frac{\prod_{v=1}^m(t-v)^{2r}}{\prod_{h=0}^n(t+h)^2},
 \qquad d=2r-1.
\]
The rational function has degree at most \(-2\), and its first \(d\)
derivatives vanish at the positive integer zeros through \(m\).
Write its partial fractions as
\[
 R(t)=\sum_{j=0}^n\left(\frac{b_j}{t+j}+\frac{c_j}{(t+j)^2}\right).
\]
An explicit pole expansion gives
\begin{align*}
 c_j&=\binom nj^2\binom{j+m}m^{2r}>0,\\
 b_j&=c_j\bigl((2r+2)H_j-2rH_{j+m}-2H_{n-j}\bigr),
 \qquad\sum_jb_j=0.
\end{align*}
The last identity is the vanishing residue at infinity. Termwise
differentiation and summation therefore give the single-target form
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal L_{r,n,m}
 &:=-\frac1{d!}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}R^{(d)}(k)=A\zeta(s)+B,\\
 A&=(s-1)\sum_jc_j,\\
 B&=-\sum_j\bigl((s-1)c_jH_j^{(s)}+b_jH_j^{(s-1)}\bigr).
\end{align*}
Indeed \(-R^{(d)}/d!\) has coefficients \(b_j\) at pole order
\(s-1\), and \((s-1)c_j\) at order \(s\); the summed \(s-1\)
coefficient is zero. No lower odd zeta value survives.

Here \(c_j\in\Z\), \(d_nb_j\in\Z\), and \(d_n^sB\in\Z\).
For the middle assertion, write
\(R(t)=\binom{t-1}m^{2r}Q_n(t)^2\), where
\(Q_n(t)=n!/\prod_{h=0}^n(t+h)\). The first Taylor coefficient
of the integer-valued binomial factor at an integer is cleared by
\(d_m\), by Vandermonde expansion; that of the regular pole factor
is cleared by \(d_n\). Since \(m\le n\), \(d_m\mid d_n\).
The harmonic denominators then prove the final assertion. Thus with
\(D=d_n^s\) and \(g=\gcd(DA,DB)>0\), the primitive form is
\((D/g)\mathcal L_{r,n,m}\). All arithmetic factors are explicit.

\subsection{Poisson summation and an explicit error bound}
Extend \(R\) by zero to the left of \(m\), and let
\(f(t)=-R^{(d)}(t)/d!\) for \(t>m\), zero otherwise.
This is continuous, decays integrably, and has integrable piecewise
derivatives. Its derivative has a finite jump at \(m\), so its
distributional second derivative is a finite measure. Moreover
\(\int_\R f=0\), since \(R^{(d-1)}(m)=0\).
Define
\[
 V=|R^{(2r)}(m)|+\int_m^\infty|R^{(2r+1)}(t)|\,dt.
\]
Integration by parts in the Fourier coefficient gives
\( |\widehat f(\ell)|\le V/(d!(2\pi\ell)^2)\) for \(\ell\ne0\).
Periodization of \(f\) converges uniformly and has these Fourier
coefficients; the displayed bound makes its Fourier series absolutely
convergent. This justifies Poisson summation for this particular
piecewise-smooth function, giving
\[
 \mathcal L_{r,n,m}
 =-\frac1{d!}\sum_{\ell\ne0}(2\pi i\ell)^d
       \int_m^\infty R(t)e^{-2\pi i\ell t}\,dt,
 \qquad
 |\mathcal L_{r,n,m}|\le\frac V{12d!}.
\]
The constant is \(\sum_{\ell\ne0}(2\pi\ell)^{-2}=1/12\).
This bound can be made entirely rational:
\begin{align*}
 V&\le W_{r,n,m},\\
 W_{r,n,m}&=
 \frac{(2r)!}{m^{2r+2}\binom{n+m}m^2}
 +(2r)!\sum_{j=0}^n\frac{|b_j|}{(m+j)^{2r+1}}
 +(2r+1)!\sum_{j=0}^n\frac{c_j}{(m+j)^{2r+2}}.
\end{align*}
The first term is the exact value of \(R^{(2r)}(m)\); integrate the
absolute partial-fraction majorant for \(R^{(2r+1)}\) to get the other
two terms. Consequently the primitive bound is
\[
 \left|\frac Dg\mathcal L_{r,n,m}\right|
       \le\frac{D W_{r,n,m}}{12g(2r-1)!}.
\]
No negative exponential rate or all-index nonvanishing is established
for this last expression. At eighteen tested parameter choices at
weights five, seven, and nine, independent rational zeta enclosures
place the primitive form outside \([-1,1]\). This finite observation
does not decide the limiting behavior. The separate construction,
integrality argument, and Poisson representation are paper results;
they are not claimed as a completed Lean irrationality theorem.

\subsection{Transferring back to the original decay family}
Useful identities from the derivative family can be used in the original
family without replacing its construction. This requires an explicit
transition, including its arithmetic cost. At a common even index
\(n\ge\max(2r,rm)\), let \((a,b)\in\Z^2\) be the primitive coefficient
pair of the derivative form, and let \(M\) be the original full cleared
Pad\'e coefficient matrix, with \(D=d_n^s\) and \(\delta=\det M\ne0\).
Define
\[
 z=(0,\ldots,0,a,b)^T,\qquad
 x=\operatorname{adj}(M)z,\qquad v=x/\delta.
\]
Then \(Mv=z\), and the two families give exactly the same primitive form:
\begin{equation}
 \frac Dg\mathcal L_{r,n,m}
 =a\zeta(s)+b
 =D\sum_{j=0}^r v_j\cA_{s,n,n-j}.
 \label{eq:cross-family-transition}
\end{equation}
If \(t\) is the least denominator of \(v\) and \(w=tv\), then
\(Mw=tz\) and the target-and-constant gcd is precisely \(t\), because
\(\gcd(a,b)=1\). Thus the transition preserves primitive normalization
exactly. It incurs the absolute mass \(\|v\|_1\), or equivalently
the weighted profile height of this particular transferred vector.
For \(s\ge7\), Proposition~\ref{prop:elementary-seven} and its
parametric extension apply with that actual height:
\[
 |a\zeta(s)+b|
 \le C_s(n+1)^{2s+2}d_n^s
       J_s(v)\,(2397\cdot4^{s-7})^{-n}.
\]
The transferred \(v\) need not be a vector in a reduced pair, so no
small-cost assertion is inferred from its existence. A quantitative
bound for this transition, or the direct derivative bound, would be a
valid way of combining the earlier estimates. Neither is established
at the required exponential rate here. Exact tests verify eighteen
primitive transition identities; their complete majorants remain above
one at those indices. No limiting conclusion is extrapolated from them.

\section{A sufficient target for arbitrary cancellation weights}
Fix any odd \(s=2r+1\ge7\), and let indices run through any unbounded
sequence of eligible even integers. Set
\[
 A_s=\log2397+(s-7)\log4,\qquad D_n=d_n^s.
\]
The fixed-weight analytic estimates above give
\(S_{s,n}\le B_s(n+1)^{2s+2}e^{-A_sn}\), with \(B_s>0\)
independent of \(n\). Choose any independent integer cancellation
vectors \(w_{1,n},w_{2,n}\in\Lambda_{s,n}\), and put
\[
 h_{i,n}=\gcd(Y(w_{i,n}),Z(w_{i,n})),\qquad
 \Theta_{s,n}=\max_{i=1,2}\frac{\|w_{i,n}\|_1}{h_{i,n}}.
\]
Full augmented rank makes each \(h_{i,n}\) positive and the two
target-and-constant pairs independent. Consequently the primitive forms
\[
 \ell_{i,n}=\frac{Y(w_{i,n})}{h_{i,n}}\zeta(s)
              +\frac{Z(w_{i,n})}{h_{i,n}}
\]
cannot both vanish. The triangle inequality gives the exact bound
\[
 \max_i|\ell_{i,n}|\le D_n S_{s,n}\Theta_{s,n}.
\]
Thus the following is a sufficient target, with no positivity restriction
on the selected weights:
\begin{equation}
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\Theta_{s,n}}n<A_s-s.
 \label{eq:arbitrary-weight-target}
\end{equation}
Indeed \(\log D_n/n\to s\), so the complete majorant has strictly
negative upper logarithmic rate. Both forms then tend to zero. If
\(\zeta(s)=p/q\), each nonzero integer-coefficient form has absolute
value at least \(1/|q|\), contradicting their joint nonvanishing.
Clearing rational weights also works: further integer scaling multiplies
the norm and its coefficient content by the same factor.

For a Gauss-reduced integral basis, the individual contents are at least
\(\Gamma_{s,n}\), and the ambient dimension is \(r+1\). Therefore
\(\Theta_{s,n}\le\sqrt{r+1}\lambda_{2,s,n}/\Gamma_{s,n}\), and an
equivalent sufficient joint rate target for this majorant is
\begin{equation}
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \frac{\log\Gamma_{s,n}-\log\lambda_{2,s,n}}n>s-A_s.
 \label{eq:arbitrary-weight-joint-target}
\end{equation}
The reduced-angle identity
\(\lambda_2^2=\kappa H/\sin\phi\), where
\(\sqrt3/2\le\sin\phi\le1\), rewrites its left side using
\(\log\Gamma-\tfrac12\log\kappa-\tfrac12\log H\).
This retains correlation between the factors.

There is a simple uniform allowance: \(A_s-s>3/4\) for every \(s\ge7\).
For an elementary exact verification, use
\(\log(1+x)>2x/(2+x)\) for \(x>0\). Applying it at
\(x=1/4,1/5,1/6,1/7\) gives
\[
 \log2>\frac29+\frac2{11}+\frac2{13}+\frac2{15}.
\]
Applying it at \(x=349/2048\) gives
\(\log(2397/2048)>698/4445\). These rational inequalities imply
\(\log2397>31/4\); the slope \(\log4-1\) is positive.
Hence either eventual estimate
\[
 \Theta_{s,n}\le e^{3n/4},\qquad\text{or}\qquad
 \Gamma_{s,n}\ge e^{-3n/4}\lambda_{2,s,n}
\]
is sufficient at every fixed odd weight. The allowance \(A_s-s\) is
approximately \(0.7819732344\) at weight seven and increases by
\(2(\log4-1)\) at each weight shift. More generally any cost rate
\(\eta_s<A_s-s\) suffices.

The Lean files prove the abstract decay implication for arbitrary
\(\eta_s\) and the uniform \(3/4\) allowance. The lattice
identifications and irrationality application in this section are paper
arguments. Neither sufficient target has been established for the actual
family here; this section does not assert unconditional odd-zeta
irrationality.

\section{Signed moment reduction and a polynomial--exponential majorant}
Fix any integer \(r\ge3\), \(s=2r+1\), and even \(n\ge2r\).
Put \(m=n-r\), \(K=3n\), \(D=d_n^s\), and
\(w(t)=t(t+n)/(12n^2)\). Let \(F\) map a target-and-constant
pair \(y=(a,b)\) to its weighted polynomial profile \(z\), so that
\[
 L(y)=D\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}T_{s,n,m}(k)
                  \sum_{i=0}^r z_i w(k)^i,\qquad z=Fy.
\]
The inverse coefficient matrix and the Newton profile map define \(F\)
over \(\Q\); full augmented rank makes its two columns independent.
This construction applies to every cancelling form in the specified span.

\begin{proposition}[A certified moment metric]
Write \(T=T_{s,n,m}\). Define, for \(0\le\ell\le2r\),
\[
 c_\ell=\sum_{m<k\le K}T(k)w(k)^\ell,\qquad
 t_\ell=\frac{T(K)((K+n)/K)^\ell K^{2\ell+1}}
 {(12n^2)^\ell(4r+1-2\ell)}.
\]
Set \(\mu=c_0+t_0\) and
\[
 G_{ij}=c_{i+j}+\mathbf1_{i=j}(r+1)t_{2i},\qquad S=F^{\mathsf T}GF.
\]
Then \(G,S\) are positive definite rational matrices and
\begin{equation}
 |L(y)|^2\le D^2\mu\,y^{\mathsf T}Sy\qquad(y\in\R^2).
 \label{eq:moment-metric-bound}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For \(t\ge2n\), logarithmic differentiation gives
\[
 (\log(t^{4r+2}T(t)))'\le-E/t+B/t^2,
 \quad E=(2r-1)n-1,\quad B=2m^2+sn(n+1).
\]
Here \(E>0\) and
\(EK-B=(4r-6)n^2+(2r-4)n-2r^2>0\).
Thus \(t^{4r+2}T(t)\) decreases beyond \(K\). Since
\(k+n\le ((K+n)/K)k\) there, comparison with the integral
of \(t^{-(4r+2-2\ell)}\) proves
\(\sum_{k>K}T(k)w(k)^\ell\le t_\ell\).
The finite squared polynomial moment retains all cross terms
\(c_{i+j}\). On the tail, Cauchy--Schwarz bounds its square by
\((r+1)\sum_i z_i^2w(k)^{2i}\). Hence the complete squared
moment is at most \(z^{\mathsf T}Gz\), while total mass is at most
\(\mu\). A second Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives
\eqref{eq:moment-metric-bound}. All sums converge by the tail estimates.
The finite sum contains at least \(r+1\) distinct positive-weight
evaluation points. A nonzero polynomial of degree at most \(r\)
cannot vanish at all of them. This proves positive definiteness of
\(G\), and independence of the columns of \(F\) proves it for \(S\).
\end{proof}

Choose \(U\in\mathrm{GL}_2(\Z)\) by rational binary Gauss reduction,
with
\[
 U^{\mathsf T}SU=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\\beta&\nu\end{pmatrix},
 \quad 0<\alpha\le\nu,\quad 2|\beta|\le\alpha.
\]
Its two columns give primitive forms that cannot both vanish, since
their coefficient determinant is \(\pm1\). Both satisfy
\[
 |L(Ue_i)|^2\le D^2\mu\nu.
\]
For every real pair \((h,k)\), the homogeneous version is
\[
 |L(U(h,k))|^2\le D^2\mu(\alpha h^2+2\beta hk+\nu k^2)
 \le\tfrac32D^2\mu\nu(h^2+k^2).
\]
The last step uses \(2|hk|\le h^2+k^2\). An estimate independent
of unbounded \((h,k)\) would contradict scaling of a nonzero form.

\begin{lemma}[Polynomial--exponential comparison]
If, for a fixed weight, constants \(C>0\), \(q\in\R\), and
\(\eta>0\) satisfy eventually
\begin{equation}
 0\le d_n^{2s}\mu_{s,n}\nu_{s,n}
       \le C(n+1)^q e^{-\eta n},
 \label{eq:moment-majorant-obligation}
\end{equation}
then the left-hand side tends to zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For \(x\to\infty\), \(x^q e^{-\eta x}\to0\).
Substitute \(x=n+1\) and multiply by \(Ce^\eta\), then apply
the squeeze theorem. This also works along any unbounded sequence of
eligible even indices.
\end{proof}
The existence of constants in \eqref{eq:moment-majorant-obligation}
has not been established for this family. In particular the analytic
bound for \(\mu\) does not bound \(\nu\). The latter still requires
an arithmetic estimate; the comparison lemma does not assume it silently.
Exact rational checks cover 42 selected forms across weights
\(7,9,11,13,15,17,21\), including index \(160\) at weight seven.
Forty certified squared bounds are below one; the two at weight
\(21\), index \(20\), exceed one. These finite checks imply no
all-index limiting assertion.
The companion Lean module \texttt{MomentMajorant.lean} checks the reduced
quadratic estimate and the majorant-to-limit implication. The actual
moment-family identification and the sufficient arithmetic majorant
remain separate from those abstract formal statements.

\section{Exact signed-prefix cancellation and the large-index equality}
The moment inequality can retain the signed finite sum before squaring.
Fix \(s=2r+1\ge7\), \(m=n-r\), and a cutoff \(K>m\). Write
\[
 m_j=\sum_{m<k\le K}T(k)w(k)^j,\quad P=m_0>0,\quad
 a_z=\sum_{j=0}^r z_jm_j,\quad
 b_z=\sum_{k>K}T(k)\sum_{j=0}^r z_jw(k)^j.
\]
Suppose \(\tau_j\) bounds the full tail moment of order \(j\).
Set \(E_z=(r+1)\sum_jz_j^2\tau_{2j}\) and
\(\mu=P+\tau_0\). Tail Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\(b_z^2\le\tau_0E_z\). The exact identity
\[
 (P+\tau_0)(a_z^2/P+E_z)-(a_z+b_z)^2
 =\frac{(\tau_0a_z-Pb_z)^2}{P\tau_0}
    +(P+\tau_0)(E_z-b_z^2/\tau_0)
\]
proves
\begin{equation}
 \left|\sum_{k>m}T(k)\sum_jz_jw(k)^j\right|^2
 \le\mu\left(\frac{a_z^2}{P}+E_z\right).
 \label{eq:signed-prefix-bound}
\end{equation}
The prefix variance identity proves
\(a_z^2/P\le\sum_{m<k\le K}T(k)(\sum_jz_jw(k)^j)^2\).
Thus the new rational matrix
\[
 \widehat G=(m_im_j/P)_{i,j=0}^r
       +(r+1)\diag(\tau_0,\tau_2,\ldots,\tau_{2r})
\]
is no larger than the earlier moment matrix in every quadratic direction.
This is a uniform algebraic improvement; it permits signed profiles.

Here the tail need not use a fixed power. For \(t\ge K>m\), monotonicity
of the individual fractions gives
\[
 t\frac{T'(t)}{T(t)}
 \le\sum_{h=1}^m\frac K{K-h}+m
       -s\sum_{j=0}^n\frac K{K+j}=-b_{s,n,K}.
\]
Indeed \(t/(t-h)\) decreases, \(t/(t+j)\) increases, and
\(t/(t+n+h)\le1\). Choose \(p=\lfloor b_{s,n,K}\rfloor>4r+1\).
Integrating the logarithmic derivative proves
\(T(t)\le T(K)(K/t)^p\). Integral comparison then gives the explicit,
complete infinite-tail certificate
\begin{equation}
 \tau_j=T(K)\frac K{p-2j-1}
             \left(\frac{K(K+n)}{12n^2}\right)^j,
 \qquad 0\le j\le2r.
 \label{eq:prefix-tail-certificate}
\end{equation}
For fixed rational \(c>1\), restrict to indices with \(K=cn\) integral.
Riemann sums give
\[
 \frac pn\longrightarrow d_s(c)
 =sc\log\frac{c+1}{c}-1-c\log\frac c{c-1}.
\]
If \(d_s(c)>0\), then \(p>4r+1\) eventually and
\[
 \frac{\tau_j}{T(cn)}\longrightarrow
 \frac c{d_s(c)}\left(\frac{c(c+1)}{12}\right)^j>0.
\]
In particular \(d_s(5/4)>1\) for every \(s\ge7\).
The elementary bounds \(\log(9/5)>4/7\) and
\(\log5\le9/4\) give \(d_7(5/4)>19/16\), and the expression
increases with \(s\). The companion Lean proof checks this uniform
positive coefficient; hence this cutoff supplies a valid tail power
for all sufficiently large indices at each fixed weight.
Consequently Stirling's formula in the exact factorial kernel proves the
all-index tail rate
\[
 \frac{\log\tau_j}{n}\longrightarrow\Phi_s(c),\qquad
 \Phi_s(c)=(s+1)c\log c+(c+2)\log(c+2)
 -(c-1)\log(c-1)-(s+1)(c+1)\log(c+1).
\]
Fixed endpoint offsets contribute only \(O(\log n)\) to the logarithm.
This evaluates the tail rate, independently of any integer reduction.

Retain the coefficient-to-profile map \(F\) and set
\(Q=D^2F^{\mathsf T}\widehat GF\). Gauss reduction over
\(\mathrm{GL}_2(\Z)\) gives entries \(\alpha,\beta,\nu\), with
\(0<\alpha\le\nu\) and \(2|\beta|\le\alpha\). Put
\[
 R_n=\mu\nu,\qquad C_n=\frac{\mu\det Q}{\alpha}.
\]
Since \(\det Q=\alpha\nu-\beta^2\), one obtains the exact estimates
\begin{equation}
 C_n\le R_n\le\frac43C_n,\qquad
 0\le\frac{\log R_n-\log C_n}{n}
       \le\frac{\log(4/3)}n\longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:prefix-large-index-equality}
\end{equation}
Their upper logarithmic rates are equal, even if neither ordinary rate
exists. No negative sign is inferred from this equality alone.
If \(H\) is the pulled-back tail matrix and \(q\) the pulled-back
prefix row, then \(Q=H+qq^{\mathsf T}/P\) and
\[
 \det Q=\det H+
 \frac{q_1^2H_{22}-2q_1q_2H_{12}+q_2^2H_{11}}P.
\]
This retains the exact off-diagonal cancellation in the determinant.

An explicit arithmetic attempt is also available. Write
\(q=(a/t,b/t)\), with \(\gcd(a,b)=1\), and put \(N=a^2+b^2\).
A centered B\'ezout solution \(ax+by=1\) has

The case \(N=1\) is immediate. The vectors \((b,-a),(x,y)\) form a

 R_n\le\mu\left(\frac1{t^2P}+\operatorname{tr}(H)N\right).
\]
This is unconditional as a basis estimate, but it does not close the
decay proof: in the recorded weight-seven case \(n=160,K=960\), its

\subsection{An adaptive cutoff gives a uniform subunit certificate}
There is a unified way to prove negative unscaled logarithms at every
index, while keeping the distinction from a negative exponential rate.
Fix \(s=2r+1\ge7\) and an eligible \(n\). Retain the same kernel and
coefficient lattice, but choose the prefix cutoff adaptively. Write
\(Q=H+qq^{\mathsf T}/P\) as above, with all clearing factors included.
Choose the unimodular coefficient basis
\[
 y_1=(1,-1),\qquad y_2=(2^s,-2^s-1),\qquad
 B=\tfrac32(2/3)^s,\quad \delta=B^2/16.
\]
Starting with \(K=2n\), double \(K\) until the tail power is admissible
and the following rational comparisons hold:
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\tau_0}{P}\le\delta,\qquad
 \mu\max_i y_i^{\mathsf T}Hy_i\le\delta^2.
 \label{eq:adaptive-prefix-rule}
\end{equation}
This is a terminating rule for every fixed \(s,n\). Indeed the kernel
has decay order \(E=(2r-1)n+4r+1>4r+2\). As \(K\to\infty\),
the logarithmic-derivative budget tends to \(E\), so its integer tail
power is at least \(E-1\) eventually. Hence
\(\tau_j=O_{s,n}(K^{-E+2j+1})\to0\) for \(0\le j\le2r\).
The fixed profile map gives \(H\to0\); both \(P\) and \(\mu\) tend
to the positive total kernel mass. Both quantities in
\eqref{eq:adaptive-prefix-rule} therefore tend to zero. No uniform bound
on the terminating cutoff is needed for this argument.

The two integer forms are the positive Dirichlet tails
\[
 \zeta(s)-1=\sum_{k\ge2}k^{-s},\qquad
 2^s\zeta(s)-(2^s+1)=\sum_{k\ge3}(2/k)^s.
\]
Integral comparison bounds them respectively by
\(2^{-s}(1+2/(s-1))\) and
\((2/3)^s(1+3/(s-1))\). For \(s\ge7\), both are at most \(B\):
the first comparison uses \((8/9)(3/4)^s\le1\).
The coefficient determinant is \(-1\). Their signed-prefix values \(a_i=qy_i\) differ from
the full forms by tails \(b_i\), where
\[
 b_i^2\le\tau_0\,y_i^{\mathsf T}Hy_i
       \le\mu\,y_i^{\mathsf T}Hy_i\le\delta^2.
\]
Consequently \(|a_i|\le B+\delta\),
\(\mu/P\le1+\delta\), and the complete squared certificates satisfy
\[
 \mu y_i^{\mathsf T}Qy_i
 \le(1+\delta)(B+\delta)^2+\delta
 \le2B^2.
\]
For the last inequality, \(0<B\le1\) gives
\(\delta\le B/16\) and \(\delta\le1/16\). Hence the preceding
expression is at most \(((17/16)^3+1/16)B^2\le2B^2\).
The second binary minimum is at most the larger value on this
unimodular basis. At the terminating cutoff, therefore,
\begin{equation}
 0<C_n\le R_n\le\tfrac92(4/9)^s<1/64
 \quad\text{at every eligible index}.
 \label{eq:adaptive-subunit-cost}
\end{equation}
Indeed \(B\le B_7=64/729\), and
\(2(64/729)^2<1/64\). In particular both unscaled logarithms
are less than \(-\log64\), uniformly in the weight and index. The exact
logarithmic sandwich equality gives the normalized statement
\[
 \limsup\frac{\log C_n}{n}
 =\limsup\frac{\log R_n}{n}\le0.
\]
Strict negativity of this common normalized rate is not established:
the right side of \eqref{eq:adaptive-subunit-cost} is a constant in
\(n\), so it does not force decay to zero. This distinction prevents
the cutoff construction from being mistaken for an irrationality proof.
The eight new Lean lemmas verify the adaptive quadratic inequalities,
the margins, neighboring form bounds, negative logarithms, the uniform
geometric-weight cap, the explicit logarithmic bound and the nonpositive upper-rate implication. The termination and family
identification are the paper arguments above. Twelve exact rational
tests across weights \(7,9,11,13,15,17,21\) satisfy the stopping rule
already at \(K=2n\); that finite observation is not extrapolated.

\subsection{The ideal interpretation and the full growing integer range}
The index \(n\) is not the dimension of \(Q_n\): this coefficient form
is binary, and \(\mu_n\) is kernel mass. Under a unimodular change
\(Q\mapsto U^{\mathsf T}QU\), the determinant and the set of values on
nonzero integer vectors are unchanged. In particular \(\alpha_n\) is
intrinsic. A new basis can prove a short-vector bound, but cannot change
this minimum. Binary Gauss reduction already attains the second minimum.

An ideal representation is available without a conjectural motivic
identification. Write
\[
 Q=\eta\begin{pmatrix}a&b/2\\b/2&c\end{pmatrix},\quad
 \Delta=4ac-b^2>0,\quad \beta=(b+\sqrt{-\Delta})/2.
\]
Here \(a,b,c\) are primitive integers, \(a>0\), and \(\eta>0\) is
rational. Since \(\beta^2-b\beta+ac=0\), the subgroup
\(I=(a,\beta)\) is an ideal of \(\Z[\beta]\). Direct multiplication gives
\[
 N(ax+\beta y)=a(ax^2+bxy+cy^2).
\]
If \(m\) is the least positive integer represented by the primitive form,
then \(\alpha=\eta m\), \(\det Q=\eta^2\Delta/4\), and
\(C=\mu\eta\Delta/(4m)\). The elementary norm bound gives only
\(m\ge1\), recovering the content bound. A discriminant or ideal-class
estimate yielding the required larger minimum has not been established.

The direct analytic reduction is more explicit. Write the entries of
\(Q_n\) as \(A_n,B_n,C_n^{\rm entry}\), and put
\[
 \theta_n=B_n/A_n,\quad h_n=\det Q_n/A_n,\quad
 r=99/100,\quad T_n=\mu_n\det Q_n\,r^{-n}.
\]
Completing the square gives
\[
 Q_n(u,v)=A_n(u+\theta_nv)^2+h_nv^2.
\]
Thus \(\alpha_n\ge T_n\) is equivalent to \(A_n\ge T_n\) and the
following finite-range inequalities, where \(\|x\|_{\Z}\) is distance
to the nearest integer:
\begin{equation}
 \|\theta_nv\|_{\Z}^2\ge\sigma_n^2(V_n^2-v^2),\qquad
 1\le |v|<V_n,\qquad
 \sigma_n^2=\frac{\det Q_n}{A_n^2},\quad
 V_n^2=\mu_nA_n r^{-n}.
 \label{eq:schur-full-range}
\end{equation}
Outside this range the second square term suffices. This targets a fixed
\(\varepsilon=-\log r>1/100\), rather than a constant improvement.

Here is the uniform asymptotic over the full growing range. Use the same
adaptive tail rule with \(\delta_n=16^{-n}\) and coordinate vectors
\((1,0),(0,1)\). For \(z=\zeta(s)\), the prefix errors have absolute
value at most \(\delta_n\); \(1\le\mu/P\le1+\delta_n\), and
\(\mu H_{ii}\le\delta_n^2\). Expanding the rank-one prefix matrix gives
\[
 z^2\le\mu A_n\le z^2+15\delta_n,\quad
 |\mu B_n-z|\le11\delta_n,\quad
 1\le\mu C_n^{\rm entry}\le1+8\delta_n,\quad
 0<\det(\mu Q_n)\le400\delta_n.
\]
Consequently
\begin{align*}
 V_n&=z(100/99)^{n/2}(1+O_s(16^{-n})),\\
 \theta_n&=z^{-1}+O_s(16^{-n}),\qquad
 \sigma_n^2\le400\cdot16^{-n}.
\end{align*}
Distance to the integers is 1-Lipschitz. Simultaneously for every integer
\(1\le|v|<V_n\), therefore,
\[
 \left|\|\theta_nv\|_{\Z}-\|v/z\|_{\Z}\right|
 \le37\delta_nV_n
 =O_s\left((\sqrt{100/99}/16)^n\right).
\]
The right side of \eqref{eq:schur-full-range} is at most
\(7600((100/99)/16)^n\). Taking minima over the same full range gives
the corresponding uniform perturbation estimate for those minima.
An arithmetic lower bound for \(\min_{1\le v<V_n}\|v/z\|_{\Z}\)
strong enough to close \eqref{eq:schur-full-range} is still unproved.
This analytic asymptotic does not itself exclude exact rational resonance.
Forty exact finite checks pass the Schur tests, but are not extrapolated.
Lean verifies the algebraic norm and Schur identities, basis invariance
and the uniform perturbation inequality; the family asymptotics are the
paper derivation here.

\subsection{Moving integer points for the arithmetic minimum}
One can use variable integer points at the actual slope \(\theta_n\).
Adjacent continued-fraction convergents \(p/q,p'/q'\) give a unimodular
pair \((-p,q),(-p',q')\). With \(V=q'\ge q\), their errors are at most
\(1/V\). The terminal rational case has final error zero; the initial
pair with denominator zero also satisfies this bound. These facts
follow from the convergent recurrence and its determinant identity.
The Schur formula therefore bounds both quadratic values by
\(A_n/V^2+h_nV^2\). Since the second binary minimum is at most the
maximum on an independent integer pair,
\begin{equation}
 R_n\le\mu_n(A_n/V_n^2+h_nV_n^2).
 \label{eq:variable-point-cost}
\end{equation}
This constructs the points rather than assuming a lower bound on
\(\alpha_n\). Put \(H_n=(A_n/h_n)^{1/4}\) and
\(\lambda_n=V_n/H_n\). The right side is exactly
\[
 \mu_n\sqrt{\det Q_n}(\lambda_n^2+\lambda_n^{-2}).
\]
Choose the adjacent convergent pair giving the smaller envelope at the
last denominator below \(H_n\) or the first above it; if the rational
expansion ends earlier, use its terminal pair. The envelope decreases
up to \(H_n\) and increases afterwards, so this is an exact selection
rule among the convergent candidates.

For the stronger adaptive tolerance \(16^{-n}\), the preceding
determinant estimate gives the quantitative bound
\[
 R_n\le20\cdot4^{-n}(\lambda_n^2+\lambda_n^{-2}).
\]
Thus it suffices to prove
\(\limsup n^{-1}\log(\lambda_n^2+\lambda_n^{-2})<\log4\).
The arithmetic balance estimate for this explicit sequence has not
been established. Forty exact checks on the earlier fixed-cutoff
metrics verify the unimodular points and \eqref{eq:variable-point-cost};
their envelope is within a factor \(2.073\) of the Gauss-optimal cost
in those cases. Those finite records are not an asymptotic assertion.
Lean verifies the quadratic point bound and balance identity, alongside
the existing independent-pair minimum theorem. No irrationality
conclusion is inferred before the stated balance estimate is proved.

\subsection{Local partial quotients and limsup closure}
Suppose consecutive convergent denominators bracket the balance scale:
\(q\le H_n\le Q\). Select \(V=q\) when \(H_n^2\le qQ\), and
\(V=Q\) otherwise. Each term in
\((V/H_n)^2+(H_n/V)^2\) is at most \(Q/q\). The recurrence
\(Q=a_n^*q+q_{\rm previous}\), with \(0\le q_{\rm previous}\le q\),
therefore proves
\[
 \lambda_n^2+\lambda_n^{-2}\le2(a_n^*+1),\qquad
 R_n\le40(a_n^*+1)4^{-n}.
\]
Taking extended-real upper limits yields the precise rate budget
\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log R_n}{n}
 \le-\log4+
       \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(a_n^*+1)}n.
\]
If the second upper limit is \(\gamma<\log4\), then for every
\(0<\varepsilon<\log4-\gamma\), the definition of limsup gives
\(R_n\le e^{-\varepsilon n}\) eventually. The constants vanish after
division by \(n\). This is a complete conditional rate implication,
not a proof that the actual quotient rate satisfies its hypothesis.
Rational expansions ending before the bracket require terminal control
as well. Both algebraic bracket inequalities are checked in Lean.

\subsection{Exact multiplier entropy and the offset threshold}
The proposed saddle-point improvement can be evaluated exactly for the
integer transformation multipliers. Let \(m_n/n\to\beta\in(0,1]\), and
\[
 W_{n,m}=\sum_{j=0}^m
 \binom{n+2m+1}{m-j}\binom{n+j}{j}.
\]
Uniform factorial estimates, with \(0\log0=0\), give
\(\log|c_j|=nE_{m/n}(j/n)+O(\log(n+1))\), where
\begin{align*}
 E_\beta(x)={}&(1+2\beta)\log(1+2\beta)
 -(\beta-x)\log(\beta-x)\\
 &-(1+\beta+x)\log(1+\beta+x)
 +(1+x)\log(1+x)-x\log x.
\end{align*}
On \(0<x<\beta\), its derivative is
\(\log((\beta-x)(1+x)/(x(1+\beta+x)))\), and its second derivative
is strictly negative. The stationary equation reduces to
\(\beta-2x-2x^2=0\), so the unique maximum is
\(x_\beta=(\sqrt{1+2\beta}-1)/2\). Comparing the sum with its largest
term, and using an integer nearest \(nx_\beta\) for the lower bound,
proves the exact all-index rate
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log W_{n,m_n}}n=c(\beta)=E_\beta(x_\beta).
\]
Equivalently, with \(t_\beta=\beta/(1+\beta+\sqrt{1+2\beta})\),
\[
 c(\beta)=(1+2\beta)\log(1+t_\beta)
 -\log(1-t_\beta)-\beta\log t_\beta.
\]
The saddle equation gives
\(c'(\beta)=\log((1+t_\beta)^2/t_\beta)>0\).
At \(\beta=1/2\), \(t_\beta=3-2\sqrt2\); the polynomial identity
\[
 (1+t)^4-16t(1-t)^2=(t^2-6t+1)^2
\]
proves \(c(1/2)=\log4\). Direct substitution also gives
\(c(1)=\log(6\sqrt3)\). Thus the exact threshold is
\(c(\beta)<\log4\) precisely for \(\beta<1/2\).

For the original fixed-weight construction \(m=n-r\), one has
\(m/n\to1\): the earlier exponential mass base \(6\sqrt3\) is sharp.
Varying a separate numerator-length offset is possible in the alternative
family, but its analytic decay and primitive arithmetic normalization
must both be retained. For example, the exact first positive kernel term is
\[
 T_{s,n,m}(m+1)=
 \frac{(n!)^s(m!)^{s-1}(n+2m+1)!}{((n+m+1)!)^{s+1}}.
\]
At \(s=7,m/n\to1/2\), Stirling's formula and the LCM rate give
\[
 \frac1n\log\bigl(d_n^7 T_{7,n,m}(m+1)\bigr)
 \longrightarrow7-\log2-12\log(3/2)>1.44.
\]
The raw positive sum is at least this term, so the smaller multiplier
rate does not itself preserve the raw LCM-decay budget. This observation
does not exclude signed cancellation or further primitive gcd savings.
This calculation does not identify \(W\) with
the local partial quotient \(a_n^*\). Four Lean lemmas verify the
stationary algebra, concavity and exact half-threshold identity; the
factorial asymptotic is the paper argument above. Detailed checks of the
five proposed routes are in \texttt{exact-multiplier-entropy.md}.
No all-index partial-quotient rate or unconditional irrationality claim
is inferred from the multiplier asymptotic.

\subsection{Exact signed moments and Newton differences}
Signed structure can be retained exactly before taking a branch bound.
For \(c_j=(-1)^j\binom{n+2m+1}{m-j}\binom{n+j}{j}\),
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^m c_jy^j
 =[z^m]\frac{(1+z)^{n+2m+1}}{(1+yz)^{n+1}}.
\]
Evaluation at \(y=1\), and then differentiation at that point, give
\[
 \sum_jc_j=\binom{2m}m,\qquad
 \sum_jc_j\binom jk
 =(-1)^k\binom{n+k}k\binom{2m-k}{m-k}\quad(0\le k\le m).
\]
For arbitrary values \(f(0),\ldots,f(m)\), finite Newton interpolation
therefore proves the exact signed transform
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{j=0}^m c_jf(j)
 =\sum_{k=0}^m(-1)^k\binom{n+k}k\binom{2m-k}{m-k}\Delta^kf(0).
 \label{eq:signed-newton-transform}
\end{equation}
This retains destructive interference without presuming that consecutive
absolute magnitudes match. For the actual branches
\[
 R_j(t)=\frac{(n!)^s}{(2m)!}
          \frac{(t-m-j)_{2m}}{(t)_{n+1}^s},
\]
each Laurent coefficient is a polynomial in \(j\) of degree at most
\(2m\); the rational constant functional is linear as well. Thus
\eqref{eq:signed-newton-transform} applies to every coefficient of the
actual form. Direct pole-jet computations and independent logarithmic
jets agree in eight cases across weights \(7,9,11\), including the
rational term. A further 1771 exact moment checks support the implementation.

For fixed \(k\), the moment multiplier has rate \(\beta\log4\) when
\(m/n\to\beta>0\). The signed mass has rate \(\log4\) at \(\beta=1\).
The actual branch amplitudes have growing degree, so their higher
differences cannot be discarded using a fixed-degree argument. They can,
however, be evaluated exactly as follows; their primitive arithmetic
normalization remains a separate calculation.
Lean verifies the generalized-binomial Vandermonde identities underlying
the unshifted and shifted signed moments. The full Newton transform and
family identification here are paper arguments. This exact signed
construction does not yet prove the required all-index cost estimate.

Repeated Pascal subtraction proves the all-order identity
\[
 \Delta^k R_0(t)=(-1)^k\frac{(n!)^s}{(2m-k)!}
       \frac{(t-m)_{2m-k}}{(t)_{n+1}^s}\qquad(0\le k\le2m).
\]
To check it, write the normalized numerator as
\(\binom{t+m-j-1}{2m}\). Each backward difference reduces both
binomial arguments by one and changes the sign. The generalized
binomial difference identity is verified in Lean over \(\mathbb Q\).
Put \(M_k=\binom{n+k}k\binom{2m-k}{m-k}\). For \(t>m\),
\[
 M_k|\Delta^kR_0(t)|=\pi_k(t)T_{s,n,m}(t),\qquad
 \pi_k(t)=\binom mk\frac{(n+1)_k(t)_{m-k}}{(t+n+1)_m}.
\]
Rising-factorial Vandermonde gives \(\sum_k\pi_k(t)=1\), and all
\(\pi_k(t)\) are nonnegative. Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{k=0}^m M_k|\Delta^kR_0(t)|=T_{s,n,m}(t).
 \label{eq:signed-newton-no-loss}
\end{equation}
The same equality holds after summation over integers \(t>m\).
Thus the raw analytic transform has no absolute multiplier loss.
This positivity statement concerns \(t>m\), whereas the arithmetic
coefficients are extracted at the nonpositive poles. It alone makes
no assertion about their primitive common divisors.

\subsection{Root formulas and an adjugate polynomial pencil}
There is an exact expression for the coefficients using only the roots
of the rational kernel. Put \(Q(t)=\prod_{h=0}^n(t+h)\),
\(\xi_h=-h\), and let \(N_m\) be the normalized numerator of \(T\).
Its roots \(\rho\) are \(v,-n-v\) for \(1\le v\le m\).
Define
\[
 L_h=\frac{N_m(\xi_h)}{Q'(\xi_h)^s},\qquad
 p_{\ell,h}=\frac{(-1)^{\ell-1}}\ell
 \left(\sum_\rho\frac1{(\xi_h-\rho)^\ell}
       -s\sum_{j\ne h}\frac1{(\xi_h-\xi_j)^\ell}\right).
\]
For \(u=s-k\), the coefficient of \((t-\xi_h)^{-k}\) is exactly
\begin{equation}
 A_{k,h}=L_h
 \sum_{e_1+2e_2+\cdots+ue_u=u}
       \prod_{\ell=1}^u\frac{p_{\ell,h}^{e_\ell}}{e_\ell!}.
 \label{eq:root-partition-laurent}
\end{equation}
For \(u=0\) the sum is one. Factor the regular part at \(\xi_h\)
into \(1+(t-\xi_h)/(\xi_h-\rho)\) and
\((1+(t-\xi_h)/(\xi_h-\xi_j))^{-s}\). Its formal logarithm
after removal of \(L_h\) is \(\sum_{\ell\ge1}p_{\ell,h}(t-\xi_h)^\ell\).
Exponentiation and coefficient extraction prove
\eqref{eq:root-partition-laurent} over \(\Q\), without choosing
analytic logarithms. Summing these coefficients and their harmonic
boundary terms expresses the full rational augmented matrix \(\Phi\)
in root differences.

Let \(B=D\Phi\), \(D=d_n^s\), \(\Delta=\det B\ne0\), and
let \(t,c\) index the target and constant rows. The adjugate columns
\(U=\operatorname{adj}(B)e_t\), \(V=\operatorname{adj}(B)e_c\)
give the polynomial vector \(W(X)=XU+V\), with exact identity
\[
 BW(X)=\Delta(0,\ldots,0,X,1)^T.
\]
All its coefficients are signed minors of the root expressions above.
For integers \(p,q\), the vector \(w=pU+qV\) has coefficient
pair \(\Delta(p,q)\). Its individual coefficient content is therefore
\(h(w)=|\Delta|\gcd(p,q)\). For coprime \(p,q\), its primitive
form is \(\operatorname{sgn}(\Delta)(p\zeta(s)+q)\), and its
majorant is exactly
\[
 \frac{DS\|pU+qV\|_1}{|\Delta|}
   =S\|\Phi^{-1}(0,\ldots,0,p,q)^T\|_1.
\]
The scalar clearing factor cancels; the inverse-matrix size remains.
For \(U_j\ne0\), let \(\eta_j=-V_j/U_j\) be the root of \(W_j\).
Then, for \(q>0\),
\begin{equation}
 \|pU+qV\|_1
 =q\sum_{U_j\ne0}|U_j|\left|\frac pq-\eta_j\right|
   +q\sum_{U_j=0}|V_j|.
 \label{eq:adjugate-root-dispersion}
\end{equation}
A weighted median of these rational roots minimizes the first sum
over real slopes. Rational choices also pay the denominator \(q\).
Moreover
\[
 \eta_i-\eta_j=\frac{U_iV_j-U_jV_i}{U_iU_j}.
\]
These identities retain the correlation between adjugate entries and
the determinant. They do not supply its required asymptotic estimate.
In fact, Jacobi's complementary-minor identity gives, for \(i<j\),
\[
 U_iV_j-U_jV_i=(-1)^{i+j+1}\Delta\det C^{(ij)},
\]
where \(C\) consists of the lower-period rows of \(B\), and
\(C^{(ij)}\) deletes columns \(i,j\). For \(U_k\ne0\), set
\(g_k=\gcd(U_k,V_k)\), \(p=-V_k/g_k\), \(q=U_k/g_k\).
Then \(p,q\) are coprime, \(w_k=0\), and
\[
 \frac{\|pU+qV\|_1}{|\Delta|}
 =\frac{\sum_{j\ne k}|\det C^{(jk)}|}{g_k}.
\]
This identifies the root choice with a sparse Cramer vector normalized
by its individual coefficient content. It is not an extra independent
gcd saving. There are 53 exact complementary-minor checks and 28
primitive root-cost checks in the six profiles.
The unmixed columns give the primitive forms \(\zeta(s)\) and \(1\),
so their own majorants cannot tend to zero. Suitable mixed pairs and
their nonvanishing still require proof. Six actual profiles and 24
exact pencil identities check the formulas. Lean verifies the general
matrix adjugate image identity; the root-partition and family arguments
here are paper proofs.

\subsection{A cancellation-preserving certificate for the adjugate forms}
The adjugate combinations can be bounded without splitting their signed
coefficients. Put \(m_0=n-r\), \(u=t(t+n)\), and
\[
 R_m(u)=\left(\frac{m_0!}{m!}\right)^2
         \prod_{v=m_0+1}^m(u-v(n+v)).
\]
The exact identity \((t-v)(t+n+v)=u-v(n+v)\) gives
\(T_{s,n,m}=T_{s,n,m_0}R_m\). For coprime \(p,q\), let
\[
 H_n(u)=\frac D\Delta\sum_j(pU_j+qV_j)R_{n-j}(u)
       =\sum_{\ell=0}^rh_\ell u^\ell.
\]
Then, exactly,
\[
 L_n=p\zeta(s)+q
   =\sum_{t>m_0}T_{s,n,m_0}(t)H_n(t(t+n)).
\]
The added early terms vanish at the integer zeros of \(R_m\).
Normalization is already included in \(H_n\); no further factor
\(D\) or \(\Gamma\) is charged.

For integer \(K\ge\max(1,m_0+1)\), define
\begin{align*}
 \alpha&=s(n+1)-2n>1,\\
 C_K&=\frac{(n!)^s}{(m_0!)^2}
             \left(1+\frac{n+m_0}K\right)^{m_0},\\
 V_K&=\sum_{\ell=0}^r|h_\ell|
             \left(1+\frac nK\right)^\ell K^{-2(r-\ell)},\\
 e_K&=\frac{C_KV_KK^{1-\alpha}}{\alpha-1}.
\end{align*}
For \(t>K\), the kernel is bounded by
\(C_Kt^{2m_0-s(n+1)}\), while
\(|H_n(t(t+n))|\le V_Kt^{2r}\). The integral comparison for
\(\sum_{t>K}t^{-\alpha}\) therefore bounds the absolute tail by
\(e_K\). Keep the finite prefix signed and put
\[
 F_K=\sum_{t=m_0+1}^KT_{s,n,m_0}(t)H_n(t(t+n)),\qquad
 R_K=|F_K|+e_K.
\]
The triangle inequality in both directions proves
\[
 |L_n|\le R_K\le |L_n|+2e_K.
\]
All certificate quantities are rational. At fixed \(n,p,q\),
\(e_K\to0\); hence doubling \(K\) eventually makes
\(e_K\le4^{-n}\), giving
\[
 |L_n|\le R_n\le |L_n|+2\,4^{-n}.
\]
The cutoff affects evaluation only, not the integer coefficients.
Thus this majorant preserves any actual decay of the forms without
introducing a positive floor. It does not prove a decaying integer-pair
selection. Eight exact certificates agree with independent zeta
enclosures; the tested raw root choices are not a successful decay
selection. Lean verifies the two-sided sharpness implication; the
kernel and tail application above is a paper argument.

\subsection{Compressing cofactor-root heights}
The full cofactor-root fractions can be replaced by shorter integer
pairs. Choose \(V_j\ne0\), which exists by invertibility, and put
\(g=\gcd(U_j,V_j)\), \(p_0=-V_j/g\), \(q_0=U_j/g\).
A signed-prefix certificate \(R_0\) gives
\[
 |p_0\zeta(s)+q_0|\le R_0,\qquad
 \sigma=-q_0/p_0,\qquad \delta=R_0/|p_0|,
\]
so \(|\zeta(s)-\sigma|\le\delta\). All these bounds use rational
kernel data and do not assume irrationality.

For consecutive continued-fraction convergents \(P_i/Q_i\) of
\(\sigma\), define
\[
 E=\max_{i=0,1}\bigl(|Q_i\sigma-P_i|+Q_i\delta\bigr).
\]
The initial \(1/0\) is allowed. Their determinant is \(\pm1\),
and therefore the primitive forms \(L_i=Q_i\zeta(s)-P_i\)
cannot both vanish. The triangle inequality gives \(|L_i|\le E\).
Minimizing \(E\) over the finite list of consecutive pairs defines
a positive rational \(E_n\), including terminal pairs. Consequently
at every eligible index there exist integers \(p,q\) such that
\[
 0<|p\zeta(s)+q|\le E_n.
\]
Classical choice may select this nonzero member because its existence
follows from the unit determinant; it does not prove \(E_n\to0\).
Lean verifies the error-transfer inequality and this independent-pair
existence implication.

For \(V=\max(Q_0,Q_1)\), the continued-fraction error bound gives
\[
 E\le V^{-1}+V\delta.
\]
Thus \(\delta_n\le Ce^{-cn}\), together with a denominator window
\(e^{an}\le V_n\le e^{bn}\), \(0<a\le b<c\), would imply
\[
 E_n\le e^{-an}+Ce^{-(c-b)n}\longrightarrow0.
\]
This uses the root-accuracy budget rather than the earlier fixed
\(4^{-n}\) metric scale. A uniform estimate for \(E_n\), or for

Ten profiles at weights \(7,9,11\) give thirty exact signed-prefix

Each inequality is checked by rational arithmetic and independent zeta

\subsection{An all-index Laurent coefficient envelope}
Let \(n\ge1\), \(0\le m\le n\), \(s\ge3\), and write
\[
 T_{s,n,m}(t)=\sum_{h=0}^n\sum_{q=1}^s\frac{A_{q,h}}{(t+h)^q},
 \qquad B_h=\binom nh^s\binom{h+m}m\binom{n-h+m}m.
\]
Choose \(\rho=1/(2(n+1))\). On \(|t+h|=\rho\), the numerator
of \((t+h)^sT(t)\) increases in absolute value by at most
\(\exp(2m\rho)\le e\) relative to its value at \(t=-h\).
For the remaining denominator factors, use
\(-\log(1-u)\le2u\) for \(0\le u\le1/2\). Their inverse
product increases by at most
\[
 \exp\bigl(2s\rho(H_h+H_{n-h})\bigr)\le e^s,
\]
since \(H_h+H_{n-h}\le n\). The absolute value at the centre is
\(B_h\), so Cauchy's coefficient estimate proves
\begin{equation}
 |A_{q,h}|\le e^{s+1}B_h[2(n+1)]^{s-q}.
 \label{eq:direct-pole-envelope}
\end{equation}
This bound holds for every index. The ratio
\[
 \frac{B_{h+1}}{B_h}
 =\left(\frac{n-h}{h+1}\right)^s
   \frac{(h+m+1)(n-h)}{(h+1)(n-h+m)}
\]
is at least one up to the midpoint, and \(B_{n-h}=B_h\).
Thus \(B_h\le B_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\).
Let \(U_n\) be the maximum absolute value among the summed zeta-row
coefficients and the rational coefficient
\(-\sum_{h,q}A_{q,h}H_{h,q}\). Summing
\eqref{eq:direct-pole-envelope} and using \(H_{h,q}\le n\) gives
\[
 U_n\le sn(n+1)e^{s+1}[2(n+1)]^{s-1}B_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}.
\]
For fixed \(s\) and \(m/n\to\beta\in[0,1]\), Stirling's formula
therefore gives the explicit envelope
\begin{equation}
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log U_n}{n}
 \le s\log2+(1+2\beta)\log(1+2\beta)-2\beta\log(2\beta),
 \label{eq:direct-pole-rate}
\end{equation}
with \(0\log0=0\), whenever \(U_n>0\) eventually.
At \(\beta=1\) this upper bound is
\((s-2)\log2+3\log3\), approximately \(6.761573\) at weight seven.
It is a coefficient estimate before primitive cancellation, and is
not a negative rate for the scalar cost. The signed analytic identity,
the coefficient envelope, and the required primitive arithmetic gain
are distinct statements. The first two are proved above; the last is
not inferred from them. Exact rational checks verify 693 convex
identities and the pole bounds in the eight actual coordinate cases.
The Cauchy and asymptotic arguments are paper proofs.

\section{Uniform analytic contraction of the moment certificates}
The analytic estimates can be strengthened without changing the blocks.
Let \(n\ge4r\), \(m=n-r\), \(T_n=T_{s,n,m}\), and
\(w_n(t)=t(t+n)/(12n^2)\). Direct cancellation of the finite products gives
\[
 \frac{T_{n+2}(t+2)}{T_n(t)}=
 \frac{((n+1)(n+2))^s}{((m+1)(m+2))^2}
 \frac{[t(t+1)]^{s+1}\prod_{j=1}^6(t+n+m+j)}
 {\prod_{j=1}^4(t+n+j)^{s+1}}.
\]
This formula holds for \(t>m\), with \(m\) increasing to \(m+2\)
in the numerator kernel. In particular the integer shift aligns the
entire positive summation range.

\begin{proposition}[Kernel and moment contraction]
Put
\[
 a_s=\frac{16^{-(s-7)}}{100},\qquad
 b_s=\frac{27\,16^{-(s-7)}}{100},\qquad
 J_{\ell,n}=\sum_{k>n-r}T_n(k)w_n(k)^\ell.
\]
For every \(r\ge3\), \(s=2r+1\), even \(n\ge4r\), and
\(0\le\ell\le2r\),
\[
 J_{0,n+2}\le a_sJ_{0,n},\qquad
 J_{\ell,n+2}\le b_sJ_{\ell,n}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
With \(x=t/n\) and \(t\ge m+1\), the displayed kernel ratio is at most
\[
 C_{s,n}f_s(x),\quad
 C_{s,n}=\frac{(1+2/n)^{3s+1}(1+3/n)^6}{(1-r/n)^4},\quad
 f_s(x)=\frac{x^{2s+2}(x+2)^6}{(x+1)^{4s+4}}.
\]
Indeed \(t\ge n/2\), so \(t(t+1)\le t^2(1+2/n)\).
The six upper factors are at most \((t+2n)(1+3/n)\), and
each lower factor is at least \(t+n\). The factorial prefactor is
at most \(n^{2s-4}(1+2/n)^{2s}/(1-r/n)^4\); all powers of \(n\) cancel.
Since \(x^2/(x+1)^4\le1/16\) and \(x+2\le2(x+1)\),
\[
 f_s(x)\le16^{-(s-7)}\frac{64x^{16}}{(x+1)^{26}}
 \le16^{-(s-7)}\frac{64\cdot8^{16}5^{10}}{13^{26}}.
\]
The last maximum occurs at \(x=8/5\), by logarithmic differentiation.
Also \(\log(1+u)\le u\) gives
\[
 C_{s,n}\le(4/3)^4\exp((12r+26)/n)
 \le(4/3)^4 e^{31/6}<2304.
\]
Here \(e<3\) suffices for the last bound. Exact integer comparison gives
\(2304\cdot64\cdot8^{16}5^{10}/13^{26}<1/100\).
This proves the pointwise kernel contraction with factor \(a_s\).

For \(k\ge n-r+1\ge3n/4\), the coordinate ratio is
\[
 \frac{w_{n+2}(k+2)}{w_n(k)}
 =\left(\frac n{n+2}\right)^2
   (1+2/k)(1+4/(k+n))<e^{5/n}.
\]
Its \(\ell\)-th power is at most \(e^{10r/n}\le e^{5/2}<27\).
Multiply the pointwise bounds and sum over the aligned ranges.
Convergence follows from the earlier moment-tail bounds.
\end{proof}

Let \(n_0=4r\), \(j=(n-n_0)/2\), and let
\(H_n=(J_{p+q,n})_{0\le p,q\le r}\) be the true moment matrix.
Iteration of the proposition gives
\[
 J_{0,n}\le a_s^jJ_{0,n_0},\qquad
 \operatorname{tr}H_n\le b_s^j\operatorname{tr}H_{n_0}.
\]
The computable certificates retain this decay. At \(K=3n\),
\(w_n(K)=1\), so
\(t_{2i}=KT_n(K)/(4r+1-4i)\). Since \(T_n(K)\le J_{0,n}\),
\begin{align*}
 \mu_n&\le(1+3n/(4r+1))a_s^jJ_{0,n_0},\\
 \operatorname{tr}G_n
 &\le b_s^j[\operatorname{tr}H_{n_0}+3n(r+1)^2J_{0,n_0}].
\end{align*}
These statements use the trace of a positive matrix, rather than an
invalid inference from entrywise inequalities to quadratic-form order.

To keep normalization explicit, let \(A_n\) be the rational coefficient
matrix before clearing denominators, so \(M_n=DA_n\). The corresponding
raw profile map is \(F_n^{\rm raw}=DF_n\). Thus
\(S_n^{\rm raw}=D^2S_n\), and its reduced second coefficient is
\(\nu_n^{\rm raw}=D^2\nu_n\). In particular
\(D^2\mu_n\nu_n=\mu_n\nu_n^{\rm raw}\) exactly.

For every raw weighted profile \(z_n\), positivity and the trace bound yield
the clean majorant
\[
 |L_n|^2\le C_r(n+1)^2(a_sb_s)^j\|z_n\|_2^2,\qquad
 C_r=3(r+1)^2J_{0,n_0}
       [\operatorname{tr}H_{n_0}+J_{0,n_0}].
\]
This is an all-weight analytic bound with a polynomial prefactor.
The selected primitive forms have profiles
\(z_n=F_n^{\rm raw}U_ne_i\). Their norms have not been bounded at the
rate needed to conclude smallness. The moment contraction therefore
strengthens the proved analytic side of the same remaining limit;
it does not establish the irrationality conclusion.

\section{Explicit residue evaluation of the raw determinant}
The determinant admits an exact contour representation for the actual
certified matrix. This gives concrete contours and residues without
assuming an unspecified Barnes phase. Fix the preceding \(s,n,m,K\),
and write the columns of \(F_n^{\rm raw}\) as \(u,v\). Set
\[
 P(z)=\sum_{i=0}^r u_i w_n(z)^i,\qquad
 Q(z)=\sum_{i=0}^r v_i w_n(z)^i,\qquad
 W(z)=\pi\cot(\pi z)T_n(z).
\]
The rational kernel has the exact gamma expression
\[
 T_n(z)=
 \frac{\Gamma(n+1)^s\Gamma(z)^{s+1}\Gamma(z+n+m+1)}
 {\Gamma(m+1)^2\Gamma(z-m)\Gamma(z+n+1)^{s+1}}.
\]
Take \(\gamma_n\) to be the positively oriented rectangle with vertices
\(m+\tfrac12\pm i\) and \(K+\tfrac12\pm i\). All gamma arguments
in its closed interior have positive real part. The only poles of
\(W\) inside are \(m+1,\ldots,K\), and their residues are \(T_n(k)\).
No pole lies on the rectangle. For every polynomial \(f\), the residue
theorem therefore evaluates the contour completely:
\[
 \frac1{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma_n}W(z)f(z)\,dz
       =\sum_{k=m+1}^K T_n(k)f(k).
\]

Put \(e_i=(r+1)t_{2i}\), the explicit diagonal tail correction.
Let \(D_{\rm fin}\), \(D_{\rm mix}\), and \(D_{\rm tail}\) denote
\begin{align*}
 D_{\rm fin}
 &=\sum_{m<k<\ell\le K}T_n(k)T_n(\ell)
      [P(k)Q(\ell)-Q(k)P(\ell)]^2,\\
 D_{\rm mix}
 &=\sum_{i=0}^r e_i\sum_{k=m+1}^K T_n(k)
      [P(k)v_i-Q(k)u_i]^2,\\
 D_{\rm tail}
 &=\sum_{0\le i<j\le r}e_i e_j(u_i v_j-v_i u_j)^2.
\end{align*}
Then the full certified determinant, including all tail terms, is
\begin{equation}
 \det S_n^{\rm raw}=D_{\rm fin}+D_{\rm mix}+D_{\rm tail}.
 \label{eq:exact-residue-determinant}
\end{equation}
Indeed \(S_n^{\rm raw}\) is the sum of the rank-one matrices with
vectors \((P(k),Q(k))\), weights \(T_n(k)\), and vectors
\((u_i,v_i)\), weights \(e_i\). Expanding its two-by-two determinant
gives one squared exterior product for each unordered pair of vectors.
Every term in \eqref{eq:exact-residue-determinant} is nonnegative.
The finite term has the explicit double contour expression
\[
 D_{\rm fin}=\frac1{2(2\pi i)^2}
 \int_{\gamma_n}\int_{\gamma_n}W(z)W(t)
          [P(z)Q(t)-Q(z)P(t)]^2\,dt\,dz,
\]
and the mixed term is
\[
 D_{\rm mix}=\sum_{i=0}^r\frac{e_i}{2\pi i}
       \int_{\gamma_n}W(z)[P(z)v_i-Q(z)u_i]^2\,dz.
\]
These expressions are evaluated by the finite sums above; they leave
no unspecified residue contribution. Exact rational calculations at
eight cases in weights \(7,9,11,13\) verify the full determinant
identity and the scalar sandwich using the actual reduced coefficient.

\subsection{What the kernel saddle evaluates}
For fixed weight the factorial kernel has phase
\[
 \phi_s(x)=-(s+1)[(x+1)\log(x+1)-x\log x]
       +(x+2)\log(x+2)-(x-1)\log(x-1),\qquad x>1.
\]
Its critical equation and second derivative are explicit:
\[
 x^{s+1}(x+2)=(x-1)(x+1)^{s+1},\qquad
 \phi_s''(x)=\frac{s+1}{x(x+1)}-
                       \frac3{(x+2)(x-1)}.
\]
For every \(s\ge7\), the second derivative changes sign once, at
\(x_c(x_c+1)=2(s+1)/(s-2)\), with \(1<x_c<2\).
Thus \(\phi_s'\) decreases before \(x_c\) and increases thereafter.
It tends to \(+\infty\) at \(1\), is negative at \(2\), and tends
to zero at infinity. Consequently there is exactly one critical point
\(x_s\in(1,x_c)\), and \(\phi_s''(x_s)<0\).
The kernel phase \(\phi_s(x)+\phi_s(y)\) has a nonsingular diagonal
Hessian at \((x_s,x_s)\). Rational root brackets and negative-Hessian
certificates accompany the eight exact evaluations.

This proves nondegeneracy for the kernel phase, but it does not evaluate
the complete determinant's exponential rate. The factor
\([P(z)Q(t)-Q(z)P(t)]^2\) depends on \(n\) through the actual inverse
coefficient matrix. It vanishes on \(z=t\), so its saddle contribution
cannot be replaced by a fixed nonzero prefactor. The certified determinant
also includes \(D_{\rm mix}\) and \(D_{\rm tail}\). The explicit
representation retains these quantities rather than assigning them
an unproved polynomial size.

\subsection{Logarithmic closure and the all-weight conclusion}
For \(d_n=\det S_n^{\rm raw}>0\) in this subsection, define
\(C_n=\mu_n d_n/\alpha_n\). Positivity gives the exact identity
\[
 \log C_n=\log\mu_n+\log d_n-\log\alpha_n.
\]
If the individual normalized logarithms converge to finite limits,
their sum converges to the corresponding sum of limits. Without those
convergence statements, a sufficient rate budget is the upper rate
of \(\log\mu_n+\log d_n\) minus the lower rate of \(\log\alpha_n\).
In particular \(\alpha_n\) is a reduced lattice minimum, not a
normalization with an established gamma-product asymptotic.

\begin{corollary}[All odd weights, conditional on the actual scalar rate]
Suppose that for every \(r\ge3\), with \(s=2r+1\), there is
\(\delta_s>0\) such that along the eligible even indices eventually
\[
 \log\mu_{s,n}+\log\det S_{s,n}^{\rm raw}
           -\log\alpha_{s,n}\le-\delta_s n.
\]
Then \(\zeta(s)\) is irrational for every odd \(s\ge7\).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Exponentiation and the proved sandwich give
\(0\le R_{s,n}\le(4/3)e^{-\delta_s n}\to0\).
The two selected primitive integer forms have squares bounded by
\(R_{s,n}\), and their unimodular coefficient pairs prevent simultaneous
vanishing. If \(\zeta(s)=p/q\) with nonzero integer \(q\), any nonzero
one of these forms has absolute value at least \(1/|q|\). For sufficiently
large eligible \(n\), both squares are less than \(1/q^2\), a contradiction.
This proof is applied separately at each fixed odd weight.
\end{proof}
The scalar rate hypothesis in this corollary is not proved here.
The supplied saddle outline states its negativity without specifying
the full prefactor and the reduced-minimum rate required to evaluate it.
An estimate \(v_p(x_n)=O(n)\) gives a factor \(p^{O(n)}\), which is
exponential for fixed \(p\), rather than a polynomial overhead.
Such valuations are not discarded in the rate budget.
The companion Lean module verifies the finite double-sum Gram identity,
the logarithmic decomposition, and the sandwich-to-limit implication
with the logarithmic bound as an explicit argument. The contour-family
identification and a negative rate for the actual scalar cost are not
claimed as Lean-verified or unconditional irrationality results.

 1000^6p(a(a+1))&=54685013669880747>0,\\
 2057^6-140\cdot3057^2\cdot57\cdot1000^3
       &=1178944474661131249>0.

\[
 (\log q)'=\frac2{x+2}+\frac1{x-1}-\frac6{x+1}
           >\frac{1000}{57}-3>0.
\]
The second integer inequality is exactly \(q(a)<1/140\), so
\(\la_5<-\log140\) is a global bound.

For the sharp certificate put
\[
 x_- =\frac{1056126503577}{10^{12}},\qquad
 x_+ =\frac{1056126503578}{10^{12}}.
\]
Exact rational evaluation gives \(p(x_-(x_-+1))<0\) and
\(p(x_+(x_++1))>0\). Thus \(x_-<x_*<x_+\), and monotonicity
of \(q\) encloses \(\la_5\) between the logarithms of these rational values.
For \(1\le u\le2\), let \(z=(u-1)/(u+1)\). The convergent series gives
\[
 0\le\log u-2\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\frac{z^{2k+1}}{2k+1}
 \le\frac{2z^{2L+1}}{(2L+1)(1-z^2)}.
\]
For any positive rational argument, first factor out an integral power of
two. Applying this formula with \(L=32\) and outward rational rounding
proves
\[
 -4.970793860358<\la_5<-4.970793860341.
\]
The signs and rational enclosures are reproduced by
\texttt{check\_high\_weight\_rates.py}. They certify a single global maximum,
without extrapolation from indices.

To bound the infinite sum, write \(T=T_{5,n,n}\). For \(t\ge2n\),
logarithmic differentiation gives
\[
 \frac{d}{dt}\log(t^2T(t))\le-\frac{E}{t}+\frac{B}{t^2},
 \qquad E=3n+3,\quad B=7n^2+5n.
\]
Indeed apply \(1/(t-v)\le1/t+2v/t^2\),
\(1/(t+n+v)\le1/t\), and \(1/(t+h)\ge1/t-h/t^2\)
to its finite logarithmic derivative. Set
\(K=\max(2n,n+1,\lceil B/E\rceil)\le3n\).
Then \(t^2T(t)\) is decreasing for \(t\ge K\), so
\(T(k)\le T(K)(K/k)^2\) there. The maximum summand \(P_n\)
is attained at some \(n+1\le k\le K\). An integral comparison of the
tail of \(\sum k^{-2}\) gives
\[
 \cA_{5,n,n}\le(2K+1)P_n\le(6n+1)P_n.
\]

For \(f(v)=v\log v-v\), \(f(0)=0\), integral comparison also gives
\[
 f(h)\le\log(h!)\le f(h)+1+\log(h+1)\qquad(h\in\Z_{\ge0}).
\]
At a maximizing index \(n+1\le k\le3n\), the exact factorial identity is
\[
 T(k)=\frac{(n!)^3((k-1)!)^6(k+2n)!}
             {(k-n-1)!((k+n)!)^6}.
\]
Apply the upper factorial bound to the ten numerator factors and the lower
bound to the denominator. The total error is at most
\(10[1+\log(5n+1)]\). In the main terms,
\(f(k-1)-f(k)\le0\) and
\(f(k-n)-f(k-n-1)\le\log(k-n)\le\log(2n)\), including \(k-n=1\).
Replacing these arguments accordingly leaves exactly
\(n\Phi(k/n)\le n\la_5\), apart from error at most \(\log(2n)\).
Consequently, for every even \(n\ge4\),
\begin{align*}
 \cA_{5,n,n}
 &\le2e^{10}n(6n+1)(5n+1)^{10}e^{\la_5n}\\
 &\le12\cdot5^{10}e^{10}(n+1)^{12}e^{\la_5n}
 \le C_0(n+1)^{12}140^{-n}.
\end{align*}
This proves the complete raw decay estimate. Section~\ref{sec:higher-decay} extends it to every
higher fixed weight. The primitive integer normalization cost remains in
\eqref{eq:bound}; no analytic step here discards that factor.
