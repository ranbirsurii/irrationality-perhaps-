\begin{abstract}
A positive moment determinant isolates any fixed odd zeta value without a
weight projector. We derive its nonvanishing, construct explicit integer
coefficients, and account for denominator clearing. For a fixed moment shift,
the raw determinant has logarithmic decay rate $\log 4$ on the square of its
size. An elementary binomial divisibility bound shows that the uniform
least-common-multiple normalization instead grows at every integer weight
$s>1$. Exact common-factor cancellation is therefore essential for this
family. The accompanying Lean file verifies the integer construction and
its conditional irrationality criterion. A Jacobi comparison gives explicit
two-sided estimates for the approximation error $\Delta/A$, uniformly in
all nonnegative shifts. The remaining condition becomes a precise bound
on the primitive target coefficient. No unconditional irrationality
theorem for all odd zeta values is established.
\end{abstract}

\section{The quantity an irrationality argument must make small}
Write a rational linear form as $L=A\alpha+B$, and choose an integer
$D>0$ such that $a=DA$ and $b=DB$ are integers. If $(a,b)\ne(0,0)$, set
$g=\gcd(|a|,|b|)$ and
\begin{equation}
 \ell=\frac{D}{g}L=\frac ag\alpha+\frac bg .
 \label{eq:primitive}
\end{equation}
Under the hypothesis $\alpha=p/q\in\Q$, every nonzero $\ell$ satisfies
$|q\ell|\ge1$. Thus arbitrarily late nonzero forms tending to zero imply
irrationality. The exact arithmetic budget is
\begin{equation}
 -\log|\ell|=-\log|L|-\log D+\log g .
 \label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}
An analytic estimate on $L$ alone does not control this budget.
The common divisor in \eqref{eq:primitive} must be proved, rather than
inferred from a rational or geometric change of coordinates.

\section{A pure-target construction at every integer weight}
Fix integers $s>1$, $a\ge0$, and $N\ge1$. Define
\begin{equation}
 \HH{k}{s}=\sum_{j=1}^k j^{-s},\qquad
 \mu_k=\zeta(s)-\HH{k}{s},\qquad
 \Delta_{N,a}^{(s)}=\det[\mu_{a+i+j}]_{0\le i,j<N}.
 \label{eq:moment}
\end{equation}
The case requested in the chat is $s=2r+1$ with $r\ge1$.

\begin{theorem}
For these parameters there are explicit $A_{N,a}^{(s)},B_{N,a}^{(s)}\in\Q$
such that
\begin{equation}
 0<\Delta_{N,a}^{(s)}
   =A_{N,a}^{(s)}\zeta(s)+B_{N,a}^{(s)}.
 \label{eq:affine}
\end{equation}
In particular, this construction isolates one target and is nonzero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Substitute $x=e^{-t}$ in the elementary gamma integral to obtain
\[
 \frac1{(j+1)^s}
 =\frac1{(s-1)!}\int_0^1 x^j(-\log x)^{s-1}\dd x .
\]
Summing nonnegative integrands gives
\begin{equation}
 \mu_k=\int_0^1 x^k w_s(x)\dd x,\qquad
 w_s(x)=\frac{(-\log x)^{s-1}}{(s-1)!(1-x)} .
 \label{eq:weight}
\end{equation}
This measure is finite: its total mass is $\zeta(s)<\infty$.
Expanding the determinant and symmetrizing its product integrals gives
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_{N,a}^{(s)}
 =\frac1{N!}\int_{(0,1)^N}
  \prod_{i<j}(x_i-x_j)^2
  \prod_{i=1}^N x_i^a w_s(x_i)\dd x_i>0 .
 \label{eq:gram}
\end{equation}
The integrand is bounded above by the product of the finite measures.
It is strictly positive when the variables lie in pairwise disjoint
closed interior intervals, proving strict positivity of the integral.

For the affine identity, put
\[
 P(x)=\det[x-\HH{a+i+j}{s}]_{0\le i,j<N}.
\]
Subtract row zero from every other row. Only row zero then depends on $x$,
so determinant multilinearity gives
$P(x)=(P(1)-P(0))x+P(0)$. Take $A=P(1)-P(0)$ and $B=P(0)$.
\end{proof}

The coefficient of the target has a useful independent expression:
\begin{equation}
 A_{N,a}^{(s)}
 =\det\left[
 \frac1{(a+i+j+1)^s}-\frac1{(a+i+j+2)^s}
 \right]_{0\le i,j<N-1}>0 .
 \label{eq:coefficient}
\end{equation}
Here an empty determinant is $1$. For $N=1$ the coefficient is $1$ directly.
For $N\ge2$, replace the monomial basis by
\[
 1,\quad(1-x),\quad x(1-x),\quad\ldots,\quad x^{N-2}(1-x).
\]
This integer basis change has determinant
$\pm1$. Only the first polynomial is nonzero at $1$, so the coefficient of
$\zeta(s)$ is the lower principal minor. Its entries are
\[
 \mu_k-2\mu_{k+1}+\mu_{k+2}=(k+1)^{-s}-(k+2)^{-s}.
\]
The same positive Gram argument proves the inequality.

\section{Explicit error estimates, uniform even for growing shifts}
Put $s=2r+1$, $r\ge1$, and write $(z)_k=z(z+1)\cdots(z+k-1)$.
The following bounds concern the actual coefficients in \eqref{eq:affine}.
\begin{theorem}
For $N>r$ and every integer $a\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{(2r-1)!}{(N)_{2r}(N+a)_{2r}}
 \le E_{N,a}:=\frac{\Delta_{N,a}^{(2r+1)}}{A_{N,a}^{(2r+1)}}
 \le\frac{(2r-1)!}{(N-r)_{2r}(N-r+a)_{2r}}.
 \label{eq:endpoint}
\end{equation}
The lower bound holds for all $N\ge1$. For every sequence $a_N\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
 N^{2r}(N+a_N)^{2r}E_{N,a_N}\longrightarrow(2r-1)!.
 \label{eq:uniformerror}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The positive-functional method of \cite{Ferguson} suggests using the
approximation error itself. The basis change preceding \eqref{eq:coefficient}
and the Schur complement of its lower block give the exact minimum
\begin{equation}
 E_{N,a}=\min_{\deg P<N,\ P(1)=1}\int_0^1P(x)^2\rho(x)\dd x,
 \quad\rho(x)=\frac{x^a(-\log x)^{2r}}{(2r)!(1-x)}.
 \label{eq:christoffel}
\end{equation}
Indeed, every admissible $P$ has the unique form $1+(1-x)Q$,
$\deg Q<N-1$; completing the square in the positive Gram matrix gives
its determinant divided by the lower-block determinant $A$.

For nonnegative integers $m,b$, the corresponding minimum for
$x^b(1-x)^m$ is
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_N^{(m,b)}(1)=\frac{m!(m+1)!}{(N)_{m+1}(N+b)_{m+1}}.
 \label{eq:jacobi}
\end{equation}
To check the constant, use the shifted Jacobi polynomials
\[
 J_j(x)=\frac{(-1)^j}{j!x^b(1-x)^m}
       \frac{\dd^j}{\dd x^j}\bigl[x^{b+j}(1-x)^{m+j}\bigr].
\]
Integrating by parts $j$ times gives orthogonality and, using the beta
integral, their squared norms
\[
 h_j=\frac{(j+m)!(j+b)!}{(2j+m+b+1)j!(j+m+b)!},
 \qquad J_j(1)=\binom{j+m}{j}.
\]
All boundary terms vanish by the endpoint zero orders. Cauchy--Schwarz in
this basis shows that the minimum is $1/\sum_{j<N}J_j(1)^2/h_j$,
with equality attained by coefficients proportional to $J_j(1)/h_j$.
The sum telescopes to
$(N)_{m+1}(N+b)_{m+1}/(m!(m+1)!)$; subtracting its values at $N=j+1$
and $N=j$ verifies each summand. This proves \eqref{eq:jacobi}.

The inequalities $1-x\le-\log x\le(1-x)/x$ imply, for
$q(x)=x^a(1-x)^{2r-1}/(2r)!$, that
$q\le\rho$ and $x^{2r}\rho\le q$.
The first gives the lower bound in \eqref{eq:endpoint} by
\eqref{eq:jacobi}. For the upper bound restrict \eqref{eq:christoffel}
to $P=x^rQ$, $\deg Q<N-r$, $Q(1)=1$; then
$\int P^2\rho\le\int Q^2q$. Apply \eqref{eq:jacobi} again.

Finally $N^k\le(N)_k\le(N+k-1)^k$ shows that the scaled expression in
\eqref{eq:uniformerror} lies between
\[
 (2r-1)!\left(\frac N{N+2r-1}\right)^{4r}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 (2r-1)!\left(\frac N{N-r}\right)^{4r}.
\]
These bounds are independent of $a\ge0$ and both tend to $(2r-1)!$.
\end{proof}

\section{Explicit denominator clearing, including its cost}
Let $M=a+2N-2$, $d_M=\lcm(1,\ldots,M)$, with $d_0=1$, and put
$Q=d_M^s$. For every $m\le M$,
\begin{equation}
 Q\HH{m}{s}
 =\sum_{j=1}^m(d_M/j)^s\in\Z .
 \label{eq:hclear}
\end{equation}
Define the integer matrix and two integers
\begin{align}
 Z_{ij}&=-\sum_{j'=1}^{a+i+j}(d_M/j')^s,\\
 U&=\det[Q+Z_{ij}]-\det[Z_{ij}],\qquad
 V=\det[Z_{ij}].
 \label{eq:integers}
\end{align}
Applying the affine determinant identity after scaling gives the exact relation
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{Q^N\Delta_{N,a}^{(s)}=U\zeta(s)+V,\qquad U,V\in\Z.}
 \label{eq:integerform}
\end{equation}
Consequently the primitive form is
$(Q^N/g)\Delta_{N,a}^{(s)}$, where $g=\gcd(|U|,|V|)$.
Writing $u=U/g>0$, it is also exactly $uE_{N,a}$. Thus
\eqref{eq:uniformerror} proves the sharp equivalent condition
\begin{equation}
 \ell_{N,a_N}\to0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \frac{u_{N,a_N}}{N^{2r}(N+a_N)^{2r}}\to0.
 \label{eq:heightcondition}
\end{equation}
This primitive-height estimate is not established here.
These definitions, including \eqref{eq:hclear} and \eqref{eq:integerform},
are proved in the accompanying Lean module for every natural $s$.
The positivity statement uses $s>1$ and is proved above in ordinary mathematics.

For $M\ge1$, its exact prime cost is $v_p(Q^N)=sN\lfloor\log_p M\rfloor$.
The scale $Q^N$ is safe, but it is expensive. To quantify this without the
prime number theorem, observe that
\begin{equation}
 \binom{M}{k}\mid d_M\quad(0\le k\le M),\qquad
 2^M\le(M+1)d_M .
 \label{eq:lcm}
\end{equation}
The case $M=0$ is immediate. For $M\ge1$, Legendre's formula gives, for every prime $p$,
\[
 v_p\binom{M}{k}
 =\sum_{\nu\ge1}
 \left(\left\lfloor\frac M{p^\nu}\right\rfloor
 -\left\lfloor\frac k{p^\nu}\right\rfloor
 -\left\lfloor\frac{M-k}{p^\nu}\right\rfloor\right)
 \le\lfloor\log_p M\rfloor=v_p(d_M).
\]
Each summand is $0$ or $1$ and is zero for $p^\nu>M$.
Summing the $M+1$ binomial coefficients proves the second inequality.
It follows that
\begin{equation}
 \log(Q^N)\ge sN\bigl(M\log2-\log(M+1)\bigr).
 \label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}

There is a proved arithmetic improvement to this uniform scale. In row $i$
all harmonic indices are at most $M_i=a+i+N-1$, so $d_{M_i}^s$ clears
that entire row. Set
\begin{equation}
 D_{\rm row}=\prod_{i=0}^{N-1}d_{M_i}^{s},\qquad
 G_{\rm row}=\prod_{i=0}^{N-1}(d_M/d_{M_i})^s.
 \label{eq:rowcost}
\end{equation}
Every quotient is integral and $G_{\rm row}D_{\rm row}=Q^N$.
Define $W(y)_{ij}=d_{M_i}^s y-\sum_{k=1}^{a+i+j}(d_{M_i}/k)^s$, and set
$U'=\det W(1)-\det W(0)$, $V'=\det W(0)$. Factoring each row gives
\begin{equation}
 D_{\rm row}\Delta=U'\zeta(s)+V',\quad
 U=G_{\rm row}U',\quad V=G_{\rm row}V',\quad
 g=G_{\rm row}\gcd(|U'|,|V'|).
 \label{eq:rowfactor}
\end{equation}
Thus $G_{\rm row}$ is a constructed common divisor. Its remaining cost is
explicit, including every prime:
\[
 v_p(D_{\rm row})=s\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\lfloor\log_p M_i\rfloor,
 \qquad
 \log D_{\rm row}\ge s\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}
     \bigl(M_i\log2-\log(M_i+1)\bigr),
\]
where a zero index contributes zero to the prime sum. Lean verifies the
integer coefficient and gcd factorizations and both finite cost formulas.
The final primitive pair is unchanged; \eqref{eq:heightcondition} still
requires control of the remaining gcd.

\section{Gcd normalization as a derived lattice operation}
Keep the integer row $(U,V)$ from \eqref{eq:integers}, and let
$g=\gcd(|U|,|V|)>0$. Place it in cochain degrees zero and one:
\[
 C^\bullet=\bigl[\Z^2\xrightarrow{[U\;V]}\Z\bigr].
\]
Write $u=U/g$, $v=V/g$, and choose integers $b,c$ with $bu+cv=1$.
The matrix
\[
 S=\begin{pmatrix}b&-v\\c&u\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \det S=1,\qquad [U\;V]S=[g\;0]
\]
gives $H^0(C)=\Z$ and $H^1(C)=\Z/g\Z$. Equivalently, the image ideal
and the zeroth Fitting ideal of its cokernel are $(U,V)=(g)$.
This is an exact integral presentation, retaining the discrete coefficient data.

For a nonzero integer $d$, the d\'ecalage operation of \cite[Section 6]{BMS}
is computed on a torsion-free complex by
\[
 (\eta_d C)^i=\{x\in d^iC^i:\partial x\in d^{i+1}C^{i+1}\}.
\]
Its degree-zero term is all of $\Z^2$ exactly when $d\mid U$ and $d\mid V$;
its degree-one term is $d\Z$. Thus, whenever these divisibilities hold,
identifying $d\Z$ with $\Z$ using the basis element $d$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \eta_d C\ \cong\ \bigl[\Z^2\xrightarrow{[U/d\;V/d]}\Z\bigr].
 \label{eq:eta}
\end{equation}
In particular, $\eta_g$ implements the primitive normalization itself.
It changes the complex: its degree-one cohomology is zero, whereas that of
$C$ is $\Z/g\Z$. Evaluating the divided row on $(\zeta(s),1)$ gives exactly
the normalized real form in \eqref{eq:primitive}.

For each prime $p$, this bounded free complex has derived completion
$\widehat C_p=[\Z_p^2\to\Z_p]$. Its torsion length is
\begin{equation}
 e_p=\operatorname{length}_{\Z_p}H^1(\widehat C_p)
     =v_p(g)=\min\{v_p(U),v_p(V)\},\qquad v_p(0)=+\infty.
 \label{eq:torsion}
\end{equation}
The valuation pair is precise tropical data for the coefficient row.
Exactly $e_p$ successive common-factor divisions by $p$ are available.
The same Smith calculation works for a complex of completed integral
coefficient sheaves on a pro-\'etale site. This identifies its cohomology
sheaves; global cohomology still depends on the space.
Identifying this coefficient complex with the Ap\'ery family's integral
regulator complex is an additional comparison.
An explicit cellular model is an interval with two vertex stalks and one
edge stalk, all $\Z$, and incidence restriction maps $-U,V$.
Its cellular coboundary is exactly $[U\;V]$. Relating this coefficient
system to a geometric tropicalization requires an integral comparison.

Combining all local lengths recovers the entire arithmetic saving:
\begin{equation}
 \log g=\sum_p e_p\log p,\qquad
 -\log|\ell|=-\log\Delta-\log(Q^N)+\sum_p e_p\log p.
 \label{eq:torsionbudget}
\end{equation}
Thus a successful geometric construction must prove a quantitative lower
bound on these particular torsion lengths while preserving the coefficient
row and its real evaluation. D\'ecalage computes the division; it does not
by itself prove that the saving in \eqref{eq:torsionbudget} is sufficient.
The precise transfer through a framed integral comparison is also elementary:
if $E\in\operatorname{GL}_2(\Z)$ and
\[
 [U\;V]E=d[A\;B],
 \qquad\text{then}\qquad
 \gcd(|U|,|V|)=|d|\gcd(|A|,|B|).
\]
Indeed, $E$ preserves the image ideal of the row, while multiplication by
$d$ multiplies its generator by $d$. Thus an identification transfers a
proved content estimate for the source row, with its multiplier retained.
This formula, including degenerate rows, is verified in Lean.

\section{The exact analytic scale and a normalization obstruction}
\begin{theorem}
For every fixed integer $s>1$ and fixed $a\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log\Delta_{N,a}^{(s)}}{N^2}=-\log4 .
 \label{eq:rate}
\end{equation}
Moreover the uniform integer normalization in \eqref{eq:integerform} satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \liminf_{N\to\infty}
 \frac{\log(Q^N\Delta_{N,a}^{(s)})}{N^2}
 \ge(s-1)\log4>0.
 \label{eq:obstruction}
\end{equation}
Thus this normalization cannot be the small-form sequence.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $\rho(x)=x^a w_s(x)$. Use $p_0=1$ and the monic shifted Chebyshev polynomials
\[
 p_j(x)=2^{1-2j}T_j(2x-1)\qquad(j\ge1).
\]
The monic basis change preserves the Gram determinant. Hadamard's inequality,
$|T_j|\le1$ on $[-1,1]$, and $\int\rho=\mu_a$ yield
\begin{equation}
 0<\Delta_{N,a}^{(s)}
 \le\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}\int_0^1p_j(x)^2\rho(x)\dd x
 \le\mu_a^N\,2^{-2(N-1)^2}.
 \label{eq:upper}
\end{equation}
This proves the required upper logarithmic limit.

For the lower bound, fix $0<\varepsilon<1/2$, let
$\lambda=1-2\varepsilon$, and let
$c_\varepsilon=\min_{[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]}\rho>0$.
Restricting \eqref{eq:gram} to this interval and making an affine substitution
gives
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_{N,a}^{(s)}
 \ge c_\varepsilon^N\lambda^{N^2} J_N,\qquad
 J_N=\det[(i+j+1)^{-1}]_{i,j<N}.
 \label{eq:lower}
\end{equation}
The Cauchy determinant formula, obtained by clearing denominators and
factoring its alternating numerator, gives
\[
 J_N=\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}
 \frac{(j!)^4}{(2j)!(2j+1)!}
 =\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}
 \frac1{(2j+1)\binom{2j}{j}^{\,2}} .
\]
Since $4^j/(2j+1)\le\binom{2j}{j}\le4^j$, the logarithm of this product is
$-N(N-1)\log4+O(N\log(N+1))$. Hence
$\liminf N^{-2}\log\Delta\ge\log\lambda-\log4$.
Let $\varepsilon$ decrease to zero to prove \eqref{eq:rate}.

Finally, $M=a+2N-2$ and \eqref{eq:cost} imply
$\liminf N^{-2}\log(Q^N)\ge s\log4$.
Adding \eqref{eq:rate} proves \eqref{eq:obstruction}.
\end{proof}

This obstruction concerns the uniform scale $Q^N$. It does not bound the
primitive common divisor $g$. For this fixed-shift family to yield
primitive forms tending to zero, the exact requirement is
\begin{equation}
 \log g-\log(Q^N)-\log\Delta_{N,a}^{(s)}\longrightarrow+\infty.
 \label{eq:remaining}
\end{equation}
In particular, \eqref{eq:obstruction} forces a saving of at least
$(s-1)\log4$ on the $N^2$ scale. A rational change of polynomial basis in
the same space multiplies both coefficients by $(\det T)^2$; after
primitive reduction it gives the same integer pair up to sign.
Improving the budget therefore requires additional arithmetic identities
or a different polynomial space or measure. A shift $a=a_N$ requires a
new asymptotic calculation; theorem \eqref{eq:rate} assumes $a$ fixed.

\section{Checks, source connections, and formalization boundary}
The exact rational audit already in the repository clears reduced
denominators and removes the entire gcd. At $s=5$, $N=20$, $a=0$, it encloses
the primitive form strictly above $1$ in absolute value. The displayed rates
$-\log|\Delta|/N^2\approx1.517981$ and
$-\log|\ell|/N^2\approx-4.772732$ are finite-index diagnostics.
They are not used in the proofs above and do not exclude eventual
arithmetic improvements.
The coefficient-complex audit gives
$\log(Q^N)/N^2\approx9.053638$ and
$\sum_p e_p\log p/N^2\approx2.762925$ at this index, recovering the same
negative budget via \eqref{eq:torsionbudget}.
At $s=9$, $N=5$, it also detects $e_{11}=2$ although $11>M=8$:
common factors can arise beyond the primes in the denominator scale.
All these common factors are included in the primitive normalization.

A separate sparse-space audit checks $700$ small exponent sets and $60$
larger examples across $s=3,5,7,9$. Every larger example at sizes $4,6,8$
has primitive absolute value greater than $1$. These finite checks make
no asymptotic assertion about other polynomial spaces.

There are genuine published Ap\'ery-family identifications. For the
classical $V_{12}$ family, \cite[Theorem 1.1, Section 5.4]{GKS} constructs
a regulator period with $LV=-12t$, $v_0=2\zeta(3)$, and
$v_1=10\zeta(3)-12$. Its operator is
\[
 L=\theta^3-t(2\theta+1)(17\theta^2+17\theta+5)+t^2(\theta+1)^3.
\]
The integral normal-function cone in \cite[Section 5]{K26} supplies a
geometric comparison target. These results identify the classical period;
an all-weight identification of our coefficient presentation and a
sufficient bound in \eqref{eq:torsionbudget} are still required.

The formal period-identification step is now complete under its explicit
Picard--Fuchs hypotheses. For $F=\sum f_nt^n$ satisfying $LF=ct$,
coefficient extraction gives $f_1=5f_0+c$ and
\[
 (n+2)^3f_{n+2}
 =(2n+3)\bigl(17(n+1)^2+17(n+1)+5\bigr)f_{n+1}
 -(n+1)^3f_n .
\]
Over any characteristic-zero field these equations determine every
coefficient from $c$ and $f_0$. Consequently
\[
 \left.
 \begin{gathered}
 LA=0,\quad LB=12t,\quad LV=-12t,\\
 A(0)=1,\quad B(0)=0,\quad V(0)=2z
 \end{gathered}
 \right\}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad V=2zA-B .
\]
Lean verifies this identity for the actual Euler differential operator
on formal power series. The published $V_{12}$ calculation supplies
$z=\zeta(3)$. A geometric source with the analogous higher-weight
equations and integral estimates has not been constructed here.
